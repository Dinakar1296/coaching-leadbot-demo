# Life: Deep Research + 30-Day Plan

> Two halves: **the science of life** (what life is, how it began, how it evolved, whether it exists elsewhere) and **how to live well** (what the world's philosophies teach, and what research says actually improves a life). Scientific findings, traditional teachings and unproven claims are kept clearly separate.

---

## Part 1: Everything You Need to Know

### A. The science of life

#### 1.1 What is "life"?

There is no single agreed definition. The most quoted is **NASA's working definition**: *"a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution"* (from NASA's Exobiology working group, championed by the chemist Gerald Joyce).

**Common characteristics of living things (the "MRS GREN" checklist):**
- **M**ovement
- **R**espiration (releasing energy)
- **S**ensitivity (responding to the environment)
- **G**rowth
- **R**eproduction
- **E**xcretion (removing waste)
- **N**utrition

**Grey areas:**
- **Viruses** reproduce and evolve but only inside host cells, so scientists argue about whether they are alive.
- **Prions** are infectious proteins.
- Some **laboratory RNA systems** evolve in a test tube.

**Vitalism**, the old belief that living things contain a special non-physical "life force," was abandoned by science as chemistry showed that living matter follows ordinary chemical laws. Friedrich Wöhler's 1828 synthesis of urea is the classic early example.

#### 1.2 Timeline of life on Earth (evidence-based)

| When | Event | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|
| **4.54 billion years ago (Ga)** | Earth forms | Strong (radiometric dating of meteorites) |
| **~4.4 Ga** | Oldest zircon crystals, hinting at liquid water | Strong |
| **~4.2 Ga** | **LUCA** (Last Universal Common Ancestor of all living things) may have lived. A 2024 study estimates it was already a fairly complex cell with about 2,500 genes | Model estimate, debated |
| **~3.7 Ga** | Possible stromatolites in Greenland | Contested |
| **~3.48 Ga** | **Stromatolites** (microbial mats) in the Dresser Formation, Western Australia | Widely accepted |
| **~2.4 Ga** | **Great Oxidation Event**: cyanobacteria fill the air with oxygen | Strong |
| **~2 to 1.5 Ga** | First eukaryotes (cells with a nucleus; mitochondria came from engulfed bacteria, the "endosymbiosis" theory) | Strong |
| **~540 Ma** | **Cambrian explosion**: most animal body plans appear | Strong |
| **~470 Ma** | Plants move onto land | Strong |
| **252 Ma** | End-Permian extinction ("Great Dying"), about 90% of marine species lost | Strong |
| **66 Ma** | Asteroid impact ends the non-bird dinosaurs | Strong (Chicxulub crater) |
| **~300,000 years ago** | *Homo sapiens* (fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco) | Strong |
| **~70,000 to 50,000 years ago** | Modern humans spread out of Africa and reach India | Strong (genetic + archaeological) |

#### 1.3 How did life begin? (abiogenesis: still unsolved)

- **Miller-Urey experiment (1952, published 1953):** sparks through a mix of gases produced **amino acids**, showing that life's building blocks can form naturally.
- **RNA world hypothesis:** RNA can both store information and act as an enzyme (a "ribozyme"), so early life may have been RNA-based before DNA and proteins.
- **Where?** Candidates are **deep-sea hydrothermal vents** (chemical energy, mineral surfaces), **warm little ponds** on land (wet-dry cycles; favoured by Darwin in 1871), and ice.
- **Panspermia** (life arriving from space on meteorites) is a hypothesis, **not proven**. Meteorites do contain amino acids, and samples from asteroid Bennu (returned 2023) contain amino acids and nucleobases, but not life.
- **Honest status:** no one has yet created life from non-living chemicals in a lab.

#### 1.4 Evolution: how life changes

- **Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace (1858); Darwin's *On the Origin of Species* (1859):** natural selection.
- **How it works:** (1) individuals vary; (2) variation is inherited (genes); (3) more are born than survive; (4) those with helpful traits leave more offspring. Over many generations, populations change.
- **Other mechanisms:** genetic drift, gene flow, sexual selection, mutation, horizontal gene transfer (common in bacteria; this is how antibiotic resistance spreads).
- **Evidence:** fossils, DNA comparisons (humans and chimpanzees share about 98 to 99% of comparable DNA), observed evolution (antibiotic-resistant bacteria, peppered moths, Darwin's finches), anatomy (homologous bones), embryology.
- **Tree of life:** three **domains**: **Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya** (Carl Woese, 1977 and 1990).
- **How many species?** About **8.7 million (±1.3 million)** eukaryotic species (Mora et al., 2011). Only about 1.2 to 2 million have been named. Bacteria and archaea may number in the millions to billions more.

#### 1.5 Extreme life and life elsewhere

- **Extremophiles** live in boiling vents (above 120°C), acid, salt, radiation and deep rock kilometres down. **Tardigrades** survive drying out, near-vacuum and extreme cold in a dormant state.
- **Habitable zone:** the region around a star where liquid water can exist on a planet's surface.
- **Best candidates in our Solar System:**
  - **Mars** (ancient lakes and rivers). In **September 2025**, NASA reported a *"potential biosignature"* in the **Sapphire Canyon** sample from the rock **Cheyava Falls** (Jezero crater): organic carbon, "leopard spot" mineral patterns that on Earth usually come from microbes, and no confirmed non-biological explanation yet. **A potential biosignature is not proof of life**; the sample would need to come back to Earth for confirmation.
  - **Europa** (Jupiter) and **Enceladus** (Saturn): oceans under ice. NASA's Europa Clipper launched in 2024.
  - **Titan** (Saturn): lakes of methane.
- **Exoplanets:** over 6,000 confirmed. Claims of biosignature gases (such as dimethyl sulfide on K2-18b in 2025) are **disputed** and not confirmed.
- **SETI** (the search for intelligent signals) has found no confirmed signal. The 1977 "Wow! signal" was never repeated.
- **Fermi paradox:** if life is common, where is everyone? This remains unanswered.

#### 1.6 The human life cycle and lifespan
- **Life expectancy at birth in India:** about **70 to 72 years** (World Bank). It was about 32 in 1947.
- **Longest verified human life:** Jeanne Calment (France), 122 years and 164 days (1875 to 1997).
- **Why we age:** accumulated DNA damage, telomere shortening, cellular senescence, mitochondrial decline, loss of protein quality control and chronic inflammation (the "hallmarks of aging").
- **What extends healthy life (strong evidence):** not smoking, physical activity, healthy weight, a good diet, moderate or no alcohol, sleep, and social connection. **No pill has been proven to extend human lifespan.**
- **"Blue Zones"** (regions with many centenarians) are **debated**: demographer Saul Newman (Ig Nobel 2024) argues many extreme-age records reflect bad record-keeping and pension fraud. The researchers who study Blue Zones say key sites have been validated. The healthy habits described there are supported by other evidence either way.

### B. How to live well

#### 1.7 What the world's traditions teach

| Tradition | Core idea of a good life | Key texts |
|---|---|---|
| **Hindu philosophy** | Four goals (**purusharthas**): **dharma** (duty, right conduct), **artha** (wealth, security), **kama** (pleasure, love), **moksha** (liberation). The Gita's **karma yoga**: act fully, without attachment to results | Upanishads, *Bhagavad Gita*, *Yoga Sutras* of Patanjali |
| **Buddhism** | **Four Noble Truths**: suffering exists; it arises from craving; it can end; the **Noble Eightfold Path** (right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration) ends it. The Middle Way between extremes | *Dhammapada*, Pali Canon |
| **Jainism** | **Ahimsa** (non-violence) toward all living beings, **anekantavada** (truth has many sides), **aparigraha** (non-possessiveness) | *Tattvartha Sutra* |
| **Sikhism** | Remember God (**naam japna**), honest work (**kirat karni**), share with others (**vand chakna**), service (**seva**) | *Guru Granth Sahib* |
| **Stoicism** (Greece, Rome) | Focus only on what you control; virtue (wisdom, courage, justice, self-control) is the only true good | Marcus Aurelius *Meditations*, Epictetus *Enchiridion*, Seneca *Letters* |
| **Aristotle** | **Eudaimonia** (flourishing) through virtuous activity and the "golden mean" | *Nicomachean Ethics* |
| **Confucianism** | Harmony through **ren** (humaneness), **li** (proper conduct), family and education | *Analects* |
| **Taoism** | Live in harmony with the **Tao** (the way). **Wu wei**: effortless action | *Tao Te Ching* |
| **Abrahamic faiths** | Love of God and neighbour, justice, mercy, charity | Torah, Bible, Qur'an |
| **Existentialism** | Life has no given meaning; we create meaning through choices and responsibility | Sartre, Camus (*The Myth of Sisyphus*), Kierkegaard |
| **Ikigai** (Japan, popularised) | A reason for being: what you love, what you're good at, what the world needs, what you can be paid for | The four-circle diagram is a Western adaptation |

#### 1.8 What research says improves a life

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Close relationships are the strongest predictor of long-term happiness and health** | The **Harvard Study of Adult Development**, running since **1938** (directed by Robert Waldinger) |
| **Loneliness is harmful** | Linked to higher risk of heart disease, dementia and early death (large meta-analyses) |
| **Exercise improves mood** | Regular activity reduces depression and anxiety symptoms, in some trials as much as standard treatments for mild to moderate depression |
| **Sleep** | Poor sleep worsens mood, decisions and health |
| **Purpose** | Having a sense of purpose is linked to better health and longer life in cohort studies |
| **Money** | Income raises life satisfaction, especially out of poverty. Above that, gains are smaller but continue in some studies |
| **Gratitude and kindness** | Small positive effects in trials. Some famous happiness-intervention studies have replicated less strongly than first claimed |
| **Meditation / mindfulness** | Moderate evidence for reducing stress, anxiety and depression relapse |
| **Nature** | Time in green spaces is linked to lower stress |

#### 1.9 Practical life skills most people are never taught
- **Money:** budget (for example the 50/30/20 split: needs/wants/savings), an emergency fund of 3 to 6 months of expenses, avoid high-interest debt (credit cards charge about 36 to 42% a year), health and term life insurance, long-term index investing, and scam awareness (UPI fraud, fake "digital arrest" calls).
- **Health:** see [06-human-body.md](06-human-body.md).
- **Relationships:** active listening, clear communication, conflict repair, and boundaries.
- **Learning:** spaced repetition, active recall, deliberate practice.
- **Time:** prioritise the important before the urgent (the Eisenhower matrix); protect deep-work time.
- **Mental health:** know the warning signs. In India, **Tele-MANAS: 14416** (free, 24x7 government mental health helpline).

#### 1.10 Claims that are unproven
- "Everything happens for a reason / your life is pre-written": a belief found in some traditions, but not testable by science.
- "The law of attraction" (thinking something makes it happen): no scientific support beyond ordinary effects of motivation.
- Longevity supplements, "anti-aging" injections and "biohacking" miracle claims: no proven lifespan extension in humans.
- "Intelligent design" as a scientific theory: rejected by the scientific community because it makes no testable predictions. Religious belief about creation is a separate matter of faith.

## Part 2: 30-Day Plan

About **45 to 60 minutes a day**: half learning the science, half practising a life skill. Keep a journal.

### Week 1: What is life? (plus self-audit)

| Day | Learn | Practise |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Definitions of life (1.1) | Write your own definition of life and of a "good life" |
| 2 | The cell as the unit of life | Life audit: rate health, relationships, work, money, meaning and fun (1 to 10) |
| 3 | DNA and heredity | Draw your family tree 3 generations back. Note health patterns |
| 4 | Metabolism: how living things get energy | Track your energy levels hourly for a day |
| 5 | Viruses: alive or not? | Write arguments for both sides |
| 6 | Timeline of life (1.2) | Make a timeline poster from 4.54 Ga to today |
| 7 | **Review** | Weekly reflection: one thing you learned, one thing you'll change |

### Week 2: Origins and evolution

| Day | Learn | Practise |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Miller-Urey and the RNA world (1.3) | Watch a video of the Miller-Urey experiment. Summarise it in 5 lines |
| 9 | Natural selection (1.4) | Explain evolution to a friend in 2 minutes |
| 10 | Evidence for evolution | Visit a natural history museum or read about human evolution |
| 11 | Human origins and the migration into India | Read about the Bhimbetka rock shelters (UNESCO site, Madhya Pradesh) |
| 12 | The three domains and biodiversity | Go outside and identify 10 species (use iNaturalist or Google Lens) |
| 13 | Extinction and conservation | Learn 3 endangered Indian species and why they're threatened |
| 14 | **Review** | Money: write your monthly budget |

### Week 3: Life beyond Earth, aging, lifespan

| Day | Learn | Practise |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Extremophiles and tardigrades (1.5) | Write about the most extreme place you'd expect life to survive |
| 16 | Mars and the Cheyava Falls biosignature | Read NASA's announcement. Note why it's "potential," not proof |
| 17 | Europa, Enceladus, exoplanets, SETI | Stargaze with a free app (Stellarium, Sky Map) |
| 18 | Aging biology (1.6) | 30-min walk. Plan a lasting habit (sleep, exercise, diet) |
| 19 | Blue Zones: habits vs data debate | Compare both sides in writing |
| 20 | Harvard happiness study (1.8) | Call or meet someone you haven't talked to in a while |
| 21 | **Review** | Emergency fund plan: how many months of expenses do you have? |

### Week 4: Philosophies of living

| Day | Learn | Practise |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | Bhagavad Gita: karma yoga (1.7) | Do one task fully today without worrying about the result |
| 23 | Buddhism: Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path | 10-min mindfulness meditation |
| 24 | Jainism and Sikhism: ahimsa, seva | Do one act of service without telling anyone |
| 25 | Stoicism: the dichotomy of control | List what worries you. Split it into "in my control" and "not in my control" |
| 26 | Aristotle, Confucius, Taoism | Choose one virtue to practise this week |
| 27 | Existentialism: creating meaning | Write what gives your life meaning |
| 28 | Ikigai and purpose | Draw your four circles |
| 29 | Research on wellbeing (1.8) | Redo the Day 2 life audit. Compare |
| 30 | **Final synthesis** | Write your personal "life philosophy" (1 page) and a 90-day plan for the area with the lowest score |
