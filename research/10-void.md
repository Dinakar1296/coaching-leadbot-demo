# The Void: Deep Research + 30-Day Plan

> "Void" means different things in physics (vacuum, empty space, cosmic voids, the quantum vacuum), in mathematics (zero, the empty set), and in philosophy and spirituality (śūnyatā, the Tao, the unmanifest). This guide covers all of them and keeps **measured facts**, **theories** and **beliefs** clearly apart.

---

## Part 1: Everything You Need to Know

### 1.1 How empty is "empty"? (measured values)

| Place | Particles per cubic centimetre (approx.) | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| Air at sea level | **~25,000,000,000,000,000,000** (2.5 × 10¹⁹) | ~101,325 Pa (1 atm) |
| Good laboratory vacuum (ultra-high vacuum) | ~25,000 to 25 million | 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻¹⁰ Pa |
| Best laboratory vacuums | ~25 | ~10⁻¹³ Pa (about 10⁻¹⁸ atm) |
| Low Earth orbit (ISS, ~400 km) | ~100 million to 1 billion | ~10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁶ Pa |
| Interplanetary space near Earth (solar wind) | **~5** (range 5 to 40, spikes to 100) | |
| Interstellar space (average) | **~1** (0.1 to 1,000 depending on region) | |
| Intergalactic space | **~1 per cubic metre** (about 10⁻⁶ per cm³) | |
| Inside large cosmic voids | Even less matter, and very few galaxies | |

**Key idea:** there is no perfect vacuum anywhere we've looked. Even intergalactic space has particles, radiation (the **cosmic microwave background**, about 400 photons per cm³ at 2.7 K), neutrinos, magnetic fields, dark matter and dark energy.

### 1.2 History of the vacuum idea

| When | Who | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| ~5th century BCE | **Leucippus and Democritus** (Greece) | Atoms moving in the void |
| ~4th century BCE | **Aristotle** | "Nature abhors a vacuum" (*horror vacui*): he argued a true void can't exist |
| Ancient India | **Vaisheshika** school (Kanada) | Atoms (*paramanu*) and **akasha** (ether/space) as a substance |
| **1643** | **Evangelista Torricelli** | Mercury barometer: the first sustained vacuum (the empty space above the mercury) |
| **1654** | **Otto von Guericke** | **Magdeburg hemispheres**: teams of horses couldn't pull apart two copper hemispheres (about 35.5 cm across) held together only by air pressure |
| 1660s | Robert Boyle | Air pump experiments: sound doesn't travel in a vacuum, flames go out |
| 1887 | **Michelson-Morley experiment** | No sign of a "luminiferous ether" filling space |
| 1905 | Einstein | Special relativity: no ether needed |
| 1920s to 40s | Quantum field theory | The vacuum is **not nothing**: fields have a lowest-energy state with fluctuations |

### 1.3 The quantum vacuum (proven effects, open questions)

- In quantum field theory, the **vacuum is the lowest-energy state of all fields**, and it still has **fluctuations**. These have measurable effects:
  - **Casimir effect:** two uncharged metal plates very close together attract each other because fewer vacuum fluctuation modes fit between them. Predicted by Hendrik Casimir in 1948; measured precisely by **Steve Lamoreaux in 1997** (within about 5% of theory at 0.6 to 6 micrometres).
  - **Lamb shift** (1947): a tiny shift in hydrogen's energy levels caused by vacuum fluctuations.
  - **Hawking radiation** (predicted 1974): black holes should slowly emit radiation because of vacuum effects near the horizon. **Not yet observed directly.**
- **"Virtual particles"** is a helpful picture used by physicists, but it's a metaphor for maths in calculations; the particles aren't little objects popping in and out that you could catch.

### 1.4 Dark energy and the cosmological constant problem (a biggest open question)

- The universe's expansion is **speeding up** (discovered 1998 using supernovae; Nobel Prize 2011).
- The cause is called **dark energy**, about **68 to 70%** of the energy in the universe. (Dark matter is about 25 to 27%, and ordinary matter only about **5%**.)
- The simplest explanation is **vacuum energy**: empty space itself has energy (Einstein's cosmological constant, Λ).
- **The problem:** quantum theory predicts a vacuum energy **up to about 120 orders of magnitude** (10¹²⁰ times) larger than what is observed. It has been called "the worst prediction in physics."
- **New in 2025:** the **DESI** survey (Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument, which mapped over 47 million galaxies and quasars) reported growing hints that **dark energy may be weakening over time**, which would mean it is not a simple constant. This is **not yet a confirmed discovery** (about 3 to 4 sigma; physicists want 5).

### 1.5 Cosmic voids: the largest empty regions

- The universe's large-scale structure is a **cosmic web**: galaxies in filaments and walls around huge **voids**.
- Voids make up most of the universe's volume. Typical voids are 30 to 300 million light-years across.
- **Boötes Void** (discovered 1981 by Robert Kirshner and colleagues): about **330 million light-years** across, with only about 60 galaxies found where thousands would be expected. Sometimes called "the Great Nothing."
- **KBC Void / Local Hole:** some astronomers argue that our Milky Way sits inside a large under-dense region about 2 billion light-years across. This might help explain the **Hubble tension** (local measurements of the expansion rate, about 73 km/s/Mpc, versus about 67 to 68 from the early universe). It is **debated**.
- **Voids are useful for science:** they are clean laboratories for testing dark energy and modified gravity.

### 1.6 The human body and the vacuum of space

- **Myth:** you would explode or instantly freeze.
- **Reality (from accidents and animal studies):**
  - You would **lose consciousness in about 10 to 15 seconds** from lack of oxygen.
  - Body fluids at body temperature start to boil (the saliva on your tongue), and skin swells (ebullism), but skin holds you together.
  - Death follows within a few minutes without repressurisation.
  - **Never hold your breath**: the lungs would rupture.
- **Real case:** on **14 December 1966**, NASA test subject **Jim LeBlanc**'s suit lost pressure in a vacuum chamber (equivalent to about 36,500 m altitude). He felt the saliva boil on his tongue, lost consciousness in about 15 seconds, and recovered fully after the chamber was repressurised about 87 seconds later.

### 1.7 Zero and the empty set (mathematics)

- **India gave the world zero as a number.**
  - **Brahmagupta's *Brahmasphutasiddhanta* (628 CE)** is the first known text to treat **zero as a number** with rules: a number minus itself is zero, a number plus zero is unchanged, and a number times zero is zero. (His rule for dividing by zero was not correct by modern standards.)
  - The **Bakhshali manuscript** uses a dot as a zero placeholder. Its date is **disputed**: 2017 radiocarbon dating suggested the 3rd to 4th century for some folios, but historians of mathematics challenged this, and a later date is widely argued.
  - Earlier, the Babylonians and Maya had placeholder symbols for "nothing here," but not zero as a number.
  - The Sanskrit word **śūnya** (empty) became the Arabic *sifr*, then Latin *zephirum*, and gave us both "**zero**" and "**cipher**."
- **The empty set ∅** (Georg Cantor, set theory, 19th century): the set with nothing in it. In modern maths, every number can be built from the empty set alone (0 = ∅, 1 = {∅}, 2 = {∅, {∅}}, ...).
- **Dividing by zero** is undefined, because no number times 0 gives a non-zero result.

### 1.8 The void in philosophy and spiritual traditions (beliefs and teachings)

| Tradition | Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Buddhism (Mahayana)** | **Śūnyatā** (emptiness) | All things are "empty" of independent, permanent self-existence; they arise dependently (*pratītyasamutpāda*). **Nāgārjuna** (about 150 to 250 CE) explained this in the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* (Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way, about 450 verses). Emptiness is **not** nothingness; the *Heart Sutra* says "form is emptiness, emptiness is form" |
| **Hindu philosophy** | **Akasha**; **Brahman**; **Nirguna** (the Absolute without attributes); "**neti neti**" ("not this, not this") | Advaita Vedanta describes ultimate reality as beyond all qualities; the *Nasadiya Sukta* (*Rigveda* 10.129) asks what existed "when there was neither existence nor non-existence" |
| **Jainism** | **Aloka-akasha** | The infinite empty space beyond the inhabited universe (*loka*) |
| **Taoism** | **Wu** (non-being), emptiness | *Tao Te Ching* ch. 11: "We shape clay into a pot, but it is the emptiness inside that makes it useful" |
| **Zen** | **Mu**, "empty mind" | Emptying the mind of fixed ideas |
| **Christian mysticism** | *Via negativa* (apophatic theology) | God is known by saying what God is not; "the cloud of unknowing" |
| **Existentialism** | Nothingness | Sartre's *Being and Nothingness* (1943): human freedom and the absence of fixed meaning |
| **Kabbalah** | **Ein Sof**, **tzimtzum** | The infinite contracting to create an "empty space" for the world |

**Meditation and the "void":** many contemplative traditions describe states of deep stillness, "no-self" or "emptiness." Brain research shows meditation changes attention and activity in the **default mode network** (the self-referential "mind-wandering" network). Researchers study these experiences, but **brain scans don't prove or disprove metaphysical claims** about what the experiences mean.

### 1.9 Myths and unproven claims

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Zero-point energy devices give free unlimited power" | **Pseudoscience.** Vacuum energy is the *lowest* state; you can't extract net energy from it in a cycle. No working device exists |
| "Space is completely empty" | False (1.1) |
| "Black holes are holes / voids" | No. They are the *densest* objects: mass packed so tightly that light can't escape |
| "You explode in space" | No (1.6) |
| "Sensory deprivation tanks open a portal to the void" | They reduce stimuli and many people find them relaxing; there's no evidence of anything beyond psychological effects |
| "Quantum vacuum proves consciousness creates reality" | A misuse of physics |

### 1.10 Education paths
- **Vacuum science and engineering:** used in semiconductors, space simulation chambers (ISRO), particle accelerators (RRCAT Indore), and coatings. Studied through physics or mechanical/electrical engineering degrees.
- **Cosmology:** M.Sc. and Ph.D. in physics or astrophysics (IUCAA Pune, RRI Bengaluru, IIA Bengaluru, TIFR, NCRA).
- **Philosophy:** Buddhist studies (Nava Nalanda Mahavihara, Central Institute of Higher Tibetan Studies at Sarnath), Indian philosophy (Sanskrit universities), Western philosophy.

## Part 2: 30-Day Plan

About **40 to 60 minutes a day**: study plus a hands-on experiment or contemplative practice.

### Week 1: The physical vacuum

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How empty is space (1.1) | Write the table from memory. Calculate: how many litres of intergalactic space hold as many particles as 1 cm³ of air? |
| 2 | Aristotle to Torricelli (1.2) | **Experiment:** fill a glass with water, cover it with a card, turn it upside down. Air pressure holds the card |
| 3 | Air pressure | **Experiment:** suck air out of a plastic bottle and watch it crush; cool a warm sealed bottle in the fridge |
| 4 | Magdeburg hemispheres | Try pulling apart two suction cups or plungers pressed together |
| 5 | Sound in a vacuum | Watch the "bell in a vacuum jar" demo (online). Why can't sound travel through a vacuum? (See [12-sound-transmission.md](12-sound-transmission.md)) |
| 6 | The body in space (1.6) | Read the Jim LeBlanc story. List what spacesuits protect against |
| 7 | **Review** | Explain to someone why space isn't truly empty |

### Week 2: The quantum vacuum and dark energy

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Fields and the quantum vacuum (1.3) | Watch a popular video on quantum fields (e.g. PBS Space Time or Fermilab's Don Lincoln) |
| 9 | Casimir effect | Read about Lamoreaux 1997. Draw the plates and the "missing" wave modes |
| 10 | Hawking radiation | Summarise it in 5 lines |
| 11 | Dark energy discovery (1998) | Read about the 2011 Nobel Prize |
| 12 | The cosmological constant problem (1.4) | Write out 10¹²⁰ and try to grasp its size (there are only about 10⁸⁰ atoms in the observable universe) |
| 13 | DESI 2025 results | Read the DESI or Fermilab press release. Note "hint" vs "discovery" |
| 14 | **Review** | Make a pie chart: dark energy / dark matter / ordinary matter |

### Week 3: Cosmic voids and zero

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | The cosmic web (1.5) | Look at the Millennium Simulation or SDSS galaxy maps |
| 16 | The Boötes Void | Find the Boötes constellation with Stellarium or a real night sky |
| 17 | KBC Void and the Hubble tension | Summarise both sides |
| 18 | Zero in India (1.7) | Read about Brahmagupta's rules. Try to explain why dividing by zero breaks things |
| 19 | The Bakhshali debate | Compare the 2017 dating claim and the historians' objections |
| 20 | The empty set | Build 0, 1, 2, 3 from ∅ on paper |
| 21 | **Review** | Write "a history of nothing" in one page |

### Week 4: The void in philosophy and practice

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | Nasadiya Sukta (*Rigveda* 10.129) | Read a translation. 10 min silent sitting |
