# Cooking from Zero: Deep Research + 30-Day Plan

> For complete beginners. It covers both **Indian home cooking** and **global basics**, because the skills (knife work, heat control, seasoning, timing) are the same everywhere. Vegetarian swaps are given for every meat dish.

---

## Part 1: Everything You Need to Know

### 1.1 How cooking actually works (the 5 fundamentals)

Every recipe is a mix of these five things. Learn them and you can cook anything without a recipe.

1. **Knife skills:** even pieces cook evenly.
2. **Heat control:** the right temperature for the right time.
3. **Seasoning:** salt, acid, fat, spice and sweetness in balance.
4. **Timing and organisation (mise en place):** everything cut and measured before you turn on the stove.
5. **Tasting:** taste at every stage and adjust.

### 1.3 Food safety (read this before anything else)

The WHO "Five Keys to Safer Food": **keep clean, separate raw and cooked, cook thoroughly, keep food at safe temperatures, use safe water and raw materials.**

#### The temperature danger zone
- **5°C to 60°C** is where bacteria grow fastest (FSSAI). At 37°C, bacteria can double every 20 minutes.
- **Keep cold food at 5°C or below** and **hot food at 60°C or above**.
- **Never leave cooked food at room temperature for more than 2 hours.** In Indian summers (35°C+), make it **1 hour**.
- **Fridge:** 1°C to 4°C. **Freezer:** -18°C.

#### Safe internal cooking temperatures (USDA, measured with a thermometer at the thickest part)

| Food | Minimum internal temperature |
|---|---|
| **Chicken and all poultry** (whole, pieces, minced) | **74°C (165°F)** |
| **Minced meat** (keema: mutton, beef, pork) | **71°C (160°F)** |
| **Whole cuts** of mutton, beef, pork (steaks, chops, roasts) | **63°C (145°F)** + rest 3 minutes |
| **Fish** | **63°C (145°F)**, or until flesh is opaque and flakes |
| **Egg dishes** | **71°C (160°F)** |
| **Leftovers and reheated food** | **74°C (165°F)**, steaming hot throughout |

#### Indian kitchen specific safety
- **Rajma (red kidney beans):** raw or undercooked beans contain a toxin (phytohaemagglutinin). **Soak 8+ hours, discard the water, then boil hard for at least 10 minutes** or pressure cook fully. Never cook rajma in a slow cooker from raw.
- **Leftover rice:** cool it quickly (spread it out), refrigerate within 1 hour, eat within 24 hours, reheat only once until steaming. Rice left warm can grow *Bacillus cereus*, whose toxin survives reheating.
- **Curd, raita, chutneys, paneer:** refrigerate. They spoil fast in heat.
- **Street-style cut fruit / salads:** wash produce in clean running water. Peel if unsure of the water quality.
- **Pressure cooker safety:** check the gasket and vent before every use. Fill **no more than 2/3** (only **1/2 for dal and beans**, which foam). Never force it open. Let pressure drop fully or use the quick-release method your model allows.

#### Other safety rules
- **Separate:** raw meat never touches ready-to-eat food. Wash the board, knife and hands (20 seconds with soap) after raw meat.
- **Thaw** in the fridge or under cold running water, never on the counter.
- **Oil fire:** **never pour water on it.** Turn off the heat and cover the pan with a lid or damp (not dripping) cloth. Keep a lid nearby when frying.
- **Knife safety:** a sharp knife is safer than a dull one (less force, less slipping). Never catch a falling knife. Put a damp cloth under your board so it doesn't slide.
- **Gas safety:** smell gas? Don't switch anything on or off. Open windows, turn off the regulator, call the gas agency.
- **Allergens:** peanuts, tree nuts (cashew, almond), milk, eggs, wheat (gluten), soy, fish, shellfish, sesame, mustard. Ask guests.

### 1.4 Knife skills

**The grip:** the **pinch grip**. Pinch the blade just in front of the handle between your thumb and the side of your index finger, and wrap the other fingers around the handle.

**The guiding hand:** the **claw**. Curl your fingertips under, so your knuckles touch the side of the blade and guide it. Your fingertips should never be under the blade.

**Motions:**
- **Rocking cut:** the knife tip stays on the board and the heel rises and falls (herbs, garlic).
- **Push cut:** slide the knife forward and down (most vegetables).
- **Pull cut:** draw the knife backwards (delicate items, tomatoes).

**Standard cuts:**

| Cut | Size | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Large dice | ~2 cm cubes | Stews, roasting, aloo sabzi |
| Medium dice | ~1.2 cm | Curries, soups |
| Small dice | ~6 mm | Salads, salsa, kachumber |
| Brunoise | ~3 mm | Fine garnishes, sauces |
| Mince | As fine as possible | Garlic, ginger, chillies |
| Julienne | Matchsticks ~3 mm x 5 cm | Stir-fry, salads, ginger garnish |
| Chiffonade | Thin ribbons of rolled leaves | Basil, spinach, mint |
| Slice / half-moon | Rounds or half-rounds | Onions for biryani, cucumbers |

**How to dice an onion:** cut it in half through the root, peel it, lay it flat, make vertical cuts toward the root (don't cut through the root), then 1 or 2 horizontal cuts, then slice across. The root holds everything together.

**Keeping knives sharp:** a **honing steel** straightens the edge (use it weekly). A **whetstone** or pull-through sharpener actually sharpens it (every 1 to 3 months). Hold the blade at about 15 to 20 degrees. Never put good knives in a dishwasher or leave them in a sink.

### 1.5 Heat: the science behind cooking

**Three ways heat moves:**
- **Conduction:** direct contact (pan to food). Searing, tawa roti.
- **Convection:** hot air or liquid moving around (oven, boiling, deep-frying, air fryer).
- **Radiation:** direct infrared heat (grill, tandoor walls, broiler, open flame roasting of baingan).

**Key reactions:**
- **Maillard reaction** (from about 140°C): proteins and sugars brown, creating deep savoury flavour. This is why seared paneer, roasted chicken, toasted bread and **bhuna masala** taste so good. It needs **dry surfaces and high heat**. Wet food steams instead of browning, so pat food dry and don't crowd the pan.
- **Caramelisation** (from about 160°C): sugar browns (caramelised onions for biryani, jaggery, caramel).
- **Protein coagulation:** proteins firm up (eggs set around 62 to 70°C). Overcooking squeezes out water, making food dry and rubbery.
- **Starch gelatinisation:** starches absorb water and swell (rice, pasta, thickening sauces with flour or cornflour).
- **Emulsification:** mixing fat and water with the help of an emulsifier (mayonnaise, vinaigrette, pasta sauces with pasta water, makhani gravy with cream).

**Cooking oils and their approximate smoke points** (these vary by brand and refining):

| Fat | Approx. smoke point | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Butter | ~150°C | Low-heat cooking, finishing, baking |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | ~190 to 210°C | Sautéing, dressings |
| Unrefined coconut oil | ~175°C | South Indian cooking, medium heat |
| Refined sunflower / rice bran / groundnut oil | ~230°C+ | Deep-frying, high-heat cooking |
| Mustard oil | ~250°C | Bengali, Punjabi, pickles (heat until it just smokes, then cool slightly, to remove pungency) |
| Ghee | ~250°C | Tadka, parathas, high-heat frying, flavour |

**Stove heat levels:**
- **High:** boiling water, searing, stir-fry, tadka (brief)
- **Medium-high:** sautéing, shallow frying
- **Medium:** most curries after the initial frying, pancakes, dosa
- **Low:** simmering, sauces, eggs, dum cooking, melting chocolate

**How to test pan temperature:** flick a drop of water. If it sizzles and evaporates, the pan is medium. If it forms a dancing ball (Leidenfrost effect), it's hot. For oil, drop in a small piece of dough or a wooden spoon handle: steady small bubbles means about 170 to 180°C, ready for frying.

### 1.6 Core cooking methods

| Method | Heat | What happens | Indian examples | Global examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Boiling** | 100°C, rolling bubbles | Fast cooking in water | Boiling potatoes, eggs, pasta-style rice | Pasta, blanched vegetables |
| **Simmering** | 85 to 95°C, gentle bubbles | Slow, even cooking | Dal, curries, rasam, kheer | Soups, stews, sauces |
| **Poaching** | 70 to 80°C, no bubbles | Delicate cooking | Poached eggs in curry | Poached eggs, fish |
| **Steaming** | Steam, 100°C | Moist, keeps nutrients | Idli, dhokla, momos, puttu, modak | Vegetables, dumplings, fish |
| **Pressure cooking** | ~115 to 120°C under pressure | 3 to 4x faster than boiling | Dal, rajma, chana, mutton, rice | Stocks, beans |
| **Sautéing / stir-frying** | High, little oil, keep moving | Quick browning | Sabzis, bhindi fry | Stir-fries, sautéed mushrooms |
| **Tadka / tempering (chhaunk)** | Very hot fat, seconds | Spices bloom and release flavour into the fat | Dal tadka, sambar, raita, poha | Chilli oil, garlic oil |
| **Bhuna** | Medium-high, stirring | Frying masala until oil separates from it | Onion-tomato masala, dry mutton bhuna | Soffritto, sofrito, mirepoix |
| **Shallow frying** | Medium-high, oil up to 1 cm deep | Crisp outside | Cutlets, tikki, fish fry, paratha | Fried eggs, schnitzel |
| **Deep frying** | 170 to 190°C, food submerged | Very crisp | Pakora, samosa, puri, vada | Fries, tempura |
| **Roasting / baking** | Oven, 160 to 230°C dry air | Browning, even cooking | Tandoori (oven), nankhatai | Roast vegetables, bread, cakes |
| **Grilling / tandoor** | Direct, very high | Char and smoky flavour | Tikka, kebabs, naan | BBQ, grilled vegetables |
| **Braising / stewing** | Sear, then simmer slowly in liquid | Tough meat becomes tender | Rogan josh, nihari, korma | Beef stew, curry |
| **Dum** | Sealed pot, low heat | Steam and aromas trapped | Dum biryani, dum aloo | Casseroles |
| **Dhungar (smoking)** | Burning charcoal + ghee in a small bowl, covered | Smoky flavour | Dal makhani, kebabs, raita | Smoked meats |
| **Fermentation** | Room temperature, time | Microbes add sourness and lightness | Idli/dosa batter, dhokla, curd, kanji | Bread, yogurt, kimchi |

### 1.7 Flavour: how to make food taste good

**The four elements** (from Samin Nosrat's *Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat*):
- **Salt:** enhances every flavour. Season in layers (a little at each stage), not all at the end. Taste before adding more.
- **Fat:** carries flavour (spices are fat-soluble, which is why tadka works) and gives richness.
- **Acid:** brightens and balances (lemon, tamarind, tomato, curd, vinegar, amchur). If food tastes "flat," it usually needs salt or acid.
- **Heat:** both temperature (browning) and chilli heat.

**Ayurveda's six tastes (shadrasa):** sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter, astringent. A balanced Indian thali includes all six.

**Umami (savoury depth):** tomatoes, onions cooked long, mushrooms, cheese, soy sauce, curry leaves, hing, fermented foods.

**The Indian spice sequence (general rule):**
1. Heat oil or ghee.
2. **Whole spices** first (cumin, mustard, bay leaf, cinnamon), until they crackle or change colour (seconds).
3. **Aromatics:** onions until golden, then ginger-garlic paste (1 to 2 minutes, until the raw smell goes).
4. **Ground spices** on lower heat for 20 to 30 seconds (they burn fast). A splash of water helps.
5. **Tomatoes / wet base**, cook until the **oil separates** (bhuna).
6. Main ingredient + liquid, then simmer.
7. **Finishing:** garam masala, kasuri methi, fresh coriander, lemon, cream or ghee at the end.

**Fixing mistakes:**

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too salty | Add more of the unsalted base (water, tomatoes, cooked potatoes, rice), or a splash of cream or curd. (A raw potato does not "absorb" salt much; it only dilutes a little.) |
| Too spicy | Add dairy (curd, cream, coconut milk), a little sugar or jaggery, lemon, or more base |
| Too sour | A pinch of sugar or jaggery, or a little cream |
| Bland | Salt first, then acid, then a pinch of garam masala or fresh herbs |
| Burnt bottom | Don't stir. Move the good top part to a new pan immediately |
| Too watery | Simmer uncovered, or add a slurry (1 tsp cornflour in 2 tbsp cold water), or mashed dal/potato |
| Too thick | Add hot water or stock a little at a time |
| Bitter spices | Spices burned. Start the tadka again |

### 1.8 Fundamental recipes and ratios

| Item | Ratio / key method |
|---|---|
| **Basmati rice (pot)** | Rinse 3 times, soak 20 to 30 min. 1 cup rice : 1.5 to 1.75 cups water. Boil, then cover and cook on low 12 minutes, rest 10 minutes. Don't stir |
| **Rice (pressure cooker)** | 1 cup rice : 2 cups water, 2 to 3 whistles for regular rice, less for soaked basmati |
| **Rice (pasta method)** | Boil in lots of salted water like pasta, drain at 90% done (best for biryani) |
| **Toor dal** | 1 cup dal : 3 cups water, pinch of turmeric, pressure cook 3 to 4 whistles. Mash, add tadka |
| **Roti dough** | 2 cups atta : about 3/4 cup water (add slowly), pinch salt, optional 1 tsp oil. Knead 5 to 8 min until soft, rest 20 to 30 min |
| **Boiled eggs** (from boiling water) | 6 min = jammy yolk, 8 min = soft-set, 10 to 11 min = hard. Then ice or cold water |
| **Pasta** | 1 litre water + 10 g salt per 100 g pasta. Cook to al dente (a little bite). Save a cup of pasta water |
| **Vinaigrette** | 3 parts oil : 1 part acid + salt, pepper, mustard (emulsifier) |
| **Béchamel (white sauce)** | 1 tbsp butter + 1 tbsp flour (roux) per 1 cup milk. Cook the roux 1 to 2 min, whisk in warm milk, simmer until thick |
| **Idli/dosa batter** | Idli: 3 to 4 parts rice (or idli rava) : 1 part urad dal (+ a few methi seeds). Dosa: similar, thinner. Soak 4 to 6 hours, grind, ferment 8 to 14 hours in a warm place |
| **Deep-frying oil** | 175 to 180°C for most things. Too low = greasy, too high = burnt outside, raw inside |
| **Curd (dahi)** | Boil milk, cool to lukewarm (about 40 to 45°C, comfortably warm to a finger), stir in 1 tsp curd per 500 ml, cover and leave in a warm place 6 to 10 hours |

**Mother sauces (French):** béchamel (milk + roux), velouté (stock + roux), espagnole (brown stock + roux), tomato, hollandaise (butter + egg yolk emulsion).

**Indian base gravies (restaurant "mother gravies"):**
- **Onion-tomato masala:** the base of most North Indian curries (make a batch and freeze it).
- **Makhani (tomato-butter-cashew):** for butter chicken, paneer makhani, dal makhani.
- **White gravy (onion, cashew, curd or cream):** for korma and shahi paneer.
- **Green gravy (spinach or coriander-mint):** palak paneer, hariyali chicken.
- **Coconut-based (South Indian):** kurma, stew, Chettinad (with roasted spices).

### 1.9 Baking basics

- **Weigh, don't scoop.** Baking is chemistry. 1 cup of flour can vary by 30%.
- **Leaveners:** **baking soda** needs acid (curd, buttermilk, lemon) and acts immediately. **Baking powder** has its own acid. **Yeast** needs time and warmth (bread, pizza).
- **Gluten:** kneading builds it (chewy bread). For cakes, mix minimally (soft crumb).
- **Oven:** always preheat 10 to 15 minutes. Most cakes bake at **175 to 180°C**. Use an oven thermometer if your oven's dial is unreliable (most are).
- **No oven?** Cakes can be steamed or baked in a pressure cooker (without the whistle and gasket, on a stand over salt or sand) or in an air fryer.

### 1.10 Nutrition and meal planning

The **ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024)** recommend "My Plate for the Day": about **half the plate vegetables and fruits**, then cereals/millets, pulses/eggs/meat/fish, nuts and seeds, and milk/curd, with limited oil, salt and sugar.

**Simple rules:**
- Include a **protein** in every meal (dal, paneer, eggs, chana, soya, curd, chicken, fish).
- Choose whole grains and **millets** (ragi, jowar, bajra) sometimes.
- **Limit salt to about 5 g a day**, sugar and deep-fried foods.
- Cook vegetables briefly and don't throw away the cooking water from dal and vegetables (nutrients).

**Meal prep strategy (saves hours):**
- Sunday: cook 1 dal, 1 batch of onion-tomato masala, chopped vegetables, dough for 2 days, boiled chana/rajma.
- Store: cooked food 3 to 4 days in the fridge; masala base 1 week in the fridge or 3 months frozen.
- Use a **weekly menu + shopping list** so you don't waste food.

### 1.11 How to read and follow any recipe

1. Read it **completely** twice before starting.
2. Check you have every ingredient and tool.
3. Do **mise en place:** chop, measure, and arrange everything in bowls.
4. Note **times and temperatures**, but trust **visual cues** (golden onions, oil separating, bubbles, colour, smell, texture).
5. Taste and adjust at the end.
6. **Scaling:** doubling ingredients is fine for most things, but spices and salt should go up by only about 1.5x first; taste, then add more. Cooking times do not double.

## Part 2: 30-Day Plan

About **1 to 1.5 hours per day**. Each day teaches **one skill + one recipe**. Vegetarian swaps are in brackets. Take a photo of every dish and write 2 lines: what went well, what to fix.

### Week 1: Foundations (knife, rice, dal, roti, eggs)

| Day | Skill focus | Recipe | Practice goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kitchen setup, food safety, pinch grip + claw | **Kachumber salad** (onion, tomato, cucumber, lemon, salt, chaat masala) | Small, even dice. 15 minutes of slow knife practice |
| 2 | Dicing onions, mincing garlic/chilli, egg heat control | **Masala omelette** + **soft scrambled eggs** (low heat) [paneer bhurji] | Two different egg textures |
