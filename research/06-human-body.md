# The Human Body: Deep Research + 30-Day Plan

> What the body is made of, how each system works, how to keep it healthy, what traditional systems (Ayurveda, yoga, Chinese medicine) teach, and which popular claims are myths. **This is education, not medical advice.** See a doctor for symptoms, and before starting new exercise if you have a health condition.

---

## Part 1: Everything You Need to Know

### 1.1 The body in numbers (evidence-based estimates)

| Measure | Typical adult value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Human cells | **~30 to 37 trillion** | 37.2 trillion (2013 estimate); ~30 trillion for a 70 kg "reference man" (Sender, Fuchs and Milo, 2016). About 84% are red blood cells by count |
| Bacterial cells living in and on you | **~38 trillion** | Ratio about **1.3 : 1** to human cells, not the old "10 : 1" myth (Sender et al., *Cell*, 2016) |
| Bones | **206** (adult) | Babies have about 270 to 300 that fuse. Some people have extra small bones |
| Skeletal muscles | **~600** | |
| Neurons in the brain | **~86 billion** | Azevedo et al., 2009. About 69 billion are in the cerebellum |
| Protein-coding genes | **~19,000 to 20,000** | The first truly complete (gapless) human genome was published in **2022** (T2T consortium): about **3.055 billion base pairs** |
| Heartbeats | ~60 to 100 per minute at rest, ~100,000 per day | |
| Blood volume | ~5 litres (about 7% of body weight) | |
| Breaths | ~12 to 20 per minute at rest | |
| Body temperature | ~36.5 to 37.5°C | Varies by person, time of day and where it's measured |
| Water | ~50 to 60% of body weight | |

### 1.2 Levels of organisation

**Atoms → molecules → cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organism.**

- **Main elements by mass:** oxygen (~65%), carbon (~18%), hydrogen (~10%), nitrogen (~3%), calcium, phosphorus.
- **The four tissue types:** epithelial (linings, skin), connective (bone, blood, fat, tendons), muscle (skeletal, cardiac, smooth), nervous (neurons and glia).
- **The cell:** membrane, nucleus (DNA), mitochondria (make ATP, the energy molecule), ribosomes (build proteins), endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi (process and ship proteins), lysosomes (recycling).
- **DNA → RNA → protein** (the "central dogma"): genes are copied into RNA, which ribosomes read to build proteins.

### 1.3 The 11 organ systems

| System | Main organs | Job | Keep it healthy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Integumentary** | Skin, hair, nails | Barrier, temperature control, vitamin D | Sun protection, hydration, don't smoke |
| **Skeletal** | Bones, cartilage, ligaments | Support, protection, blood cell production (marrow), calcium store | Weight-bearing exercise, calcium, vitamin D, protein |
| **Muscular** | Skeletal, heart and smooth muscle | Movement, posture, heat | Strength training 2+ days/week, protein |
| **Nervous** | Brain, spinal cord, nerves | Control, sensing, thinking | Sleep, exercise, learning, avoid head injury, manage blood pressure |
| **Endocrine** | Pituitary, thyroid, adrenals, pancreas, ovaries/testes | Hormones: growth, metabolism, stress, reproduction | Sleep, healthy weight, regular check-ups (thyroid, blood sugar) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Heart, blood vessels, blood | Transport oxygen, nutrients, waste | Aerobic exercise, low salt, no smoking, control BP and cholesterol |
| **Lymphatic / immune** | Lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, white blood cells | Defence against infection, fluid balance | Vaccination, sleep, nutrition, hygiene |
| **Respiratory** | Nose, trachea, lungs, diaphragm | Gas exchange (O₂ in, CO₂ out) | No smoking, avoid polluted air (use AQI apps, masks on bad days), exercise |
| **Digestive** | Mouth, oesophagus, stomach, intestines, liver, pancreas | Break down food, absorb nutrients | Fibre, water, varied diet, safe food |
| **Urinary** | Kidneys, bladder | Filter blood, control water, salt and pH | Hydration, control BP and sugar, avoid overusing painkillers |
| **Reproductive** | Ovaries, uterus / testes, prostate | Reproduction, hormones | Safe sex, screening, check-ups |

### 1.4 How the key systems actually work

**Heart and circulation:** the heart has 4 chambers. The right side pumps blood to the lungs; the left side pumps oxygen-rich blood to the body. Blood pressure is written as systolic/diastolic, e.g. 120/80 mmHg. Consistently **140/90 or higher** is hypertension by most guidelines (some, like the American guidelines, use 130/80).

**Lungs:** about 300 to 500 million alveoli (tiny air sacs) give a gas-exchange surface of roughly 50 to 100 m².

**Brain:** the **cerebrum** (thinking, senses, movement; left and right hemispheres; frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobes), **cerebellum** (coordination, balance), **brainstem** (breathing, heart rate, consciousness). The **autonomic nervous system** has a sympathetic branch ("fight or flight") and a parasympathetic branch ("rest and digest").

**Digestion:** the mouth (mechanical chewing + amylase starts on starch) → stomach (acid + pepsin for protein) → small intestine (most absorption; bile from the liver emulsifies fat; pancreatic enzymes) → large intestine (water absorption, gut bacteria ferment fibre) → out.

**Immunity:** **innate** (fast, general: skin, inflammation, neutrophils, macrophages) and **adaptive** (slow, specific, with memory: B cells make antibodies, T cells kill infected cells). Vaccines train adaptive immunity safely.

**Hormones and homeostasis:** the body keeps temperature, blood sugar, water and pH within narrow ranges using **negative feedback** (e.g. high blood sugar → pancreas releases insulin → cells take up sugar → sugar falls).

**Metabolism:** food energy is converted to ATP. Basal metabolic rate is typically ~1,200 to 1,800 kcal/day for adults, depending on size, sex, age and muscle.

### 1.5 The five pillars of health (what the evidence supports)

1. **Movement.** WHO (2020): adults should do **150 to 300 minutes of moderate** aerobic activity or **75 to 150 minutes of vigorous** activity per week, plus **muscle-strengthening on 2 or more days** a week. Sit less; any movement is better than none.
2. **Nutrition.** ICMR-NIN (2024): about half the plate vegetables and fruit, whole grains and millets, pulses/eggs/fish/meat, nuts, dairy; limit salt (**under 5 g a day**), sugar and ultra-processed food.
3. **Sleep.** Adults need about **7 to 9 hours**. Poor sleep raises the risk of obesity, diabetes, heart disease, depression and accidents.
4. **Stress and mental health.** Chronic stress raises cortisol and blood pressure. Exercise, social connection, meditation, therapy and time outdoors help.
5. **Avoid toxins.** No tobacco (including gutka and bidis), limit alcohol (no amount is fully "safe"), and reduce exposure to polluted air.

**Body weight for Indians:** because South Asians develop diabetes and heart disease at lower weights, the Indian consensus uses **BMI 23 to 24.9 = overweight** and **25 or more = obese** (WHO international cut-offs are 25 and 30). A January 2025 Indian definition (AIIMS, Fortis C-DOC and others) adds **waist circumference and health effects**, not just BMI. Waist over about **90 cm (men) or 80 cm (women)** signals abdominal obesity in Indians.

### 1.6 Health screening (typical adult check-ups in India; ask your doctor)

- Blood pressure (at least yearly)
- Fasting blood sugar or HbA1c (India has one of the world's largest diabetic populations; start screening from age 30 or earlier with risk factors)
- Lipid profile (cholesterol)
- BMI and waist circumference
- Eye and dental check-ups
- Cancer screening by age and sex: oral cavity (especially for tobacco users), breast, cervical (HPV/Pap)
- Thyroid function, vitamin D and B12, and haemoglobin (anaemia is very common in India)

### 1.7 First aid every adult should know

- **CPR (adult, 2025 AHA guidelines):** check the scene is safe, check for response and breathing, **call for help (112 in India)**, push hard and fast in the centre of the chest at **100 to 120 compressions per minute**, depth **at least 5 cm but not more than 6 cm**, allowing full recoil. Use an AED as soon as one arrives.
- **Choking (adult):** encourage coughing; if they can't breathe, give up to 5 back blows then up to 5 abdominal thrusts, and repeat.
- **Bleeding:** firm direct pressure with a clean cloth.
- **Burns:** cool running water for **20 minutes**. No ice, butter or toothpaste.
- **Stroke signs (BE FAST):** Balance, Eyes, Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call for help.
- **Heart attack signs:** chest pain or pressure, pain in the arm/jaw/back, sweating, breathlessness, nausea. Call immediately.
- Get certified: Indian Red Cross Society, St John Ambulance India, and many hospitals run 1-day BLS courses.

### 1.8 What traditional systems teach (traditional frameworks, not anatomical findings)

These systems are culturally important and some practices (yoga, meditation, breathing exercises) have good research support for specific benefits. Their **theories of the body** (doshas, chakras, meridians) are **not** found in anatomy or physiology and are not scientifically validated.

| System | Core model of the body | What research supports |
|---|---|---|
| **Ayurveda** (India) | Three **doshas** (vata, pitta, kapha), seven **dhatus** (tissues), **agni** (digestive fire), **ojas** | Some herbs have studied effects (e.g. turmeric/curcumin, ashwagandha) but evidence quality varies. A 2008 *JAMA* study found about **1 in 5** Ayurvedic products bought online contained detectable **lead, mercury or arsenic** (about 40% of *rasa shastra* products), so buy only tested, licensed products |
| **Yoga** (India) | Five **koshas** (sheaths), **nadis** (energy channels), **chakras**, **prana** | Asana, pranayama and meditation practice improves flexibility, balance, stress and blood pressure in many studies |
| **Traditional Chinese Medicine** | **Qi** flowing in **meridians**, yin and yang, five phases | Acupuncture shows modest effects for some pain conditions in trials. Meridians have no anatomical structure |
| **Siddha / Unani** (India) | Siddha: 96 principles, three humours. Unani: four humours (Greek origin) | Recognised under India's AYUSH ministry; limited high-quality trials |

### 1.9 Myths and unproven claims (debunked or unsupported)

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "We use only 10% of our brain" | False. Brain scans show all regions are active across a day |
| "Detox teas and cleanses remove toxins" | Your liver and kidneys already do this. Cleanses have no proven extra benefit |
| "Left-brained vs right-brained personalities" | Myth. Both hemispheres work together for almost everything |
| "Bacteria outnumber your cells 10 to 1" | Outdated. It's about 1.3 to 1 |
| "You lose most heat through your head" | No. Heat loss is roughly proportional to exposed surface area |
| "Cracking knuckles causes arthritis" | Studies have not found this link |
| "Alkaline water changes your blood pH" | Blood pH is tightly controlled by your lungs and kidneys |
| "You need 8 glasses of water a day exactly" | Needs vary with climate, activity and food. Drink to thirst, more in heat |

### 1.10 Careers and education
- **Medicine (MBBS)** via NEET-UG (5.5 years including internship), then MD/MS.
- **Nursing, physiotherapy, pharmacy, nutrition and dietetics, public health (MPH), biomedical science, sports science.**
- **AYUSH degrees** (BAMS for Ayurveda, BUMS, BSMS, BNYS) also go through NEET.
- **Self-learning:** anatomy and physiology textbooks and free courses (see Part 3).

## Part 2: 30-Day Plan

About **1 hour of study + 30 to 45 minutes of body practice** each day. Keep a "body journal": sleep hours, steps, meals, mood and one thing learned.

### Week 1: Cells, skeleton, muscles

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Body numbers and levels of organisation (1.1, 1.2) | Measure your baseline: weight, waist, resting heart rate, blood pressure (at a pharmacy if needed), sleep hours |
| 2 | The cell, DNA → RNA → protein | Draw a labelled cell from memory. 20-min walk |
| 3 | Skeletal system: bone types, the 206 bones by region | Find 20 bones on your own body. Bodyweight squats 3 x 10 |
| 4 | Joints: hinge, ball-and-socket, pivot, and so on | Mobility routine: neck, shoulders, hips, knees, ankles (10 min) |
| 5 | Muscular system: how contraction works (actin, myosin, ATP) | Push-ups, rows (with a backpack) and planks, 3 sets each |
| 6 | Tendons, ligaments, posture | Check your desk and phone posture. Set up screen height |
| 7 | **Review** | Quiz yourself on 30 terms. 30-min walk. Rest |

### Week 2: Heart, lungs, blood, immunity

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Heart anatomy and blood flow | Take your pulse at the wrist and neck. Calculate your resting HR |
| 9 | Blood pressure, blood vessels, cholesterol | Read up on the 140/90 threshold. Plan a lower-salt day |
| 10 | Blood: red cells, white cells, platelets, plasma, haemoglobin | Plan iron-rich meals (dal, greens, jaggery, eggs, meat) + vitamin C |
| 11 | Respiratory system, gas exchange, the alveoli | Practise diaphragmatic breathing: 5 min, 4 seconds in, 6 seconds out |
| 12 | Aerobic fitness and VO₂max | 20 to 30 min brisk walk or cycle at a "can talk, can't sing" pace |
| 13 | Immune system: innate vs adaptive, vaccines | Check your vaccination record (tetanus booster every 10 years, and so on) |
| 14 | **Review** | Watch a CPR video and practise the compression rhythm on a pillow (100 to 120/min; try the beat of "Stayin' Alive") |

### Week 3: Brain, nerves, hormones, senses

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Neurons, synapses, neurotransmitters | Draw a neuron. 10-min meditation (breath focus) |
| 16 | Brain regions and what they do | Try a balance challenge (stand on one leg with eyes closed, 30 sec each side): cerebellum |
| 17 | Autonomic nervous system, stress response | Box breathing (4-4-4-4) for 5 min when stressed |
| 18 | Sleep science: circadian rhythm, sleep stages, melatonin | Fixed bedtime, no screens 30 min before bed, dark room. Track it |
| 19 | Endocrine system: insulin, thyroid, cortisol, adrenaline | Learn the signs of diabetes (thirst, frequent urination, fatigue). Plan a sugar check if you're over 30 |
| 20 | The senses: eye, ear, taste, smell, touch | 20-20-20 rule for your eyes (every 20 min, look 20 ft away for 20 sec) |
| 21 | **Review** | Yoga session (30 min: sun salutations + stretches). Rest |

### Week 4: Digestion, kidneys, reproduction, whole-body health

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | Digestive tract step by step | Eat a high-fibre day (30 g: dal, vegetables, fruit, whole grains) |
| 23 | Liver and pancreas | Learn what alcohol does to the liver |
| 24 | Gut microbiome | Add fermented foods (curd, idli, dosa, kanji) and more fibre |
| 25 | Kidneys and urinary system, hydration | Track water intake and urine colour (pale yellow = well hydrated) |
| 26 | Reproductive system and hormones (menstrual cycle, testosterone, oestrogen) | Read reliable sources (WHO, NHS). Book any screening you're due for |
| 27 | Traditional systems (1.8) + myths (1.9) | Write 5 claims you've heard and check each one against the evidence |
| 28 | First aid (1.7) | Book a certified BLS/first-aid course (Indian Red Cross, St John Ambulance, hospitals) |
| 29 | Putting it together: homeostasis | Map one "feedback loop" (blood sugar or temperature) on paper |
| 30 | **Final review** | Re-measure your Day 1 numbers. Compare. Write a personal health plan for the next 90 days |
