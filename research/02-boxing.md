# Entry-Level Boxing: Deep Research + 30-Day Plan

> For complete beginners training for fitness, self-defence or as a first step toward amateur competition. **Safety first:** see a doctor before starting if you have heart, blood pressure, eye (especially retina), joint or neurological problems. **Do not spar (hit or get hit in the head) during these 30 days.** Learn technique first, then spar under a qualified coach.

---

## Part 1: Everything You Need to Know

### 1.1 What boxing is

Boxing is a combat sport where two people punch with padded gloves, **above the belt and on the front and sides of the body and head only**, in timed rounds. Good boxing is about **balance, distance, timing, and defence**, not just power.

**Benefits:** excellent cardio, full-body strength, coordination, reflexes, stress relief, confidence, discipline and basic self-defence.

**Amateur vs professional:**

| | Amateur (Olympic-style) | Professional |
|---|---|---|
| Governing bodies | **World Boxing** internationally. In India, the **Boxing Federation of India (BFI)**, recognised national federation and World Boxing member | WBC, WBA, IBF, WBO + national commissions |
| Rounds | Usually **3 rounds x 3 minutes**, 1 minute rest | 4 to 12 rounds x 3 minutes (women's pro rounds are usually 2 minutes) |
| Protection | Mouthguard, groin protector. Headgear for youth and many amateur levels | Mouthguard, groin, no headgear |
| Scoring | 10-point must system per round, judges score clean punches, control and activity | 10-point must system |
| Goal | Points, clean technique | Points, knockouts |

**Indian inspiration:** Vijender Singh (Olympic bronze 2008, first Indian boxing Olympic medal), M.C. Mary Kom (Olympic bronze 2012, six-time world champion), Lovlina Borgohain (Olympic bronze Tokyo 2020), Nikhat Zareen (world champion 2022 and 2023), and Jaismine Lamboria (57 kg) and Minakshi Hooda (48 kg), world champions at Liverpool in September 2025.

**Boxing styles (you will develop one over years):**
- **Out-boxer:** long range, jab and move (Muhammad Ali)
- **Swarmer / pressure fighter:** close range, constant pressure, body work (Joe Frazier)
- **Counter-puncher:** waits, makes you miss, punishes (Floyd Mayweather)
- **Boxer-puncher:** mix of skill and power (Canelo Álvarez)

### 1.3 How to wrap your hands (standard 180 inch wrap)

1. Put the **thumb loop** over your thumb, with the wrap coming over the **back of your hand** (not the palm).
2. Wrap the **wrist 3 times** (snug, not tight enough to tingle).
3. Go across the back of the hand and wrap the **knuckles 3 times** (fingers spread).
4. Go back to the wrist, then **between each finger**: from the wrist, over the back of the hand, between the pinky and ring finger, back around to the wrist, and repeat for the ring/middle and middle/index gaps.
5. **Thumb:** wrap once around the base of the thumb to lock it.
6. Criss-cross over the knuckles and back of hand a few times ("X" pattern) for padding.
7. Finish with the remaining wrap **around the wrist** and close the Velcro.
8. **Check:** make a fist. It should feel firm and supported but your fingers must not go numb or cold.

Wraps protect the small bones in your hand (a "boxer's fracture" is a break of the neck of the 5th, sometimes 4th, metacarpal, typically from untrained punching of hard objects; trained boxers more often hurt the 2nd and 3rd knuckles) and stabilise your wrist.

### 1.4 The stance (everything starts here)

**Orthodox** (right-handed): left foot and left hand forward. **Southpaw** (left-handed): right foot and right hand forward. Put your **stronger hand at the back** because it is your power hand.

**How to set up (orthodox):**
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, then step your **left foot forward** and your **right foot back** so your heels are roughly on two parallel railway tracks (not on one line, which kills balance).
2. **Lead foot** points forward or slightly inward (about 15 to 30 degrees). **Rear foot** is angled about 45 degrees, with the **rear heel slightly raised**.
3. **Knees slightly bent**, weight about **50/50** (or slightly more on the back foot), on the balls of your feet.
4. **Hips and shoulders turned** about 45 degrees (smaller target, ready to rotate).
5. **Hands up:** lead fist at eye/cheek level about 15 to 20 cm in front of your face; rear fist touching your **cheek/chin**.
6. **Elbows in**, protecting your ribs and liver.
7. **Chin down**, tucked slightly, and eyes looking through your eyebrows at the opponent's chest (to see hands and feet).
8. **Relax** your shoulders. Tension makes you slow and tired.

**Checks:** a partner gently pushes you from the front, back and sides. You should not lose balance.

### 1.5 Footwork

**Golden rules:**
- **Never cross your feet.**
- **The foot closest to where you want to go moves first** ("step and drag"). Going forward: lead foot steps, rear foot follows. Going back: rear foot steps, lead foot follows. Right: right foot first. Left: left foot first.
- Keep your **stance width the same** after every step.
- Small steps. Stay on the balls of your feet.

**Basic footwork drills:**
1. **Step-drag** forward, back, left, right (in a square pattern, 1 step each direction).
2. **Pivot:** on the ball of your lead foot, swing your rear foot around to change the angle (orthodox: pivot on the lead foot and swing the rear foot so you turn to your left, ending up outside the opponent's lead shoulder and away from their right hand).
3. **Circle:** step-drag laterally around a centre point (a cone or bag).
4. **In and out:** step in with a jab, step out immediately.
5. **Rope line drill:** lay a rope on the floor and move along and across it while keeping your stance.

### 1.6 The six punches (number system)

Coaches call punches by numbers. For **orthodox**:

| # | Punch | Hand | Key technique | Common mistakes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Jab** | Lead | Straight from the guard, rotate your fist palm-down at the end, **shoulder rises to protect your chin**, small step forward optional. Snap it back to guard as fast as it goes out | Dropping the rear hand, pawing without snap, reaching (leaning over the front foot), letting the elbow flare |
| **2** | **Cross / straight right** | Rear | **Push off the rear foot, pivot the rear heel out** ("squash the bug"), rotate hips and shoulders, extend straight. Lead hand returns to protect your face. Chin behind the shoulder | Arm-only punch with no hip rotation, looping it, dropping the lead hand, overextending |
| **3** | **Lead hook** | Lead | Elbow bent about 90 degrees, **arm parallel to the floor** (for a head hook), pivot the lead foot and hip, power comes from rotation, not the arm. Fist palm-down or thumb-up | Winding up (telegraphing), swinging wide, opening the elbow too much, not pivoting |
| **4** | **Rear hook** | Rear | Same as the lead hook with the rear hand, shorter range. Pivot the rear foot | Squaring up and losing your guard |
| **5** | **Lead uppercut** | Lead | Dip slightly by bending the knees (don't drop the hand), drive upward from the legs and hips, palm facing you, elbow stays close. Short range | Dropping the hand to "load," looping it from too far away |
| **6** | **Rear uppercut** | Rear | Drop the rear shoulder slightly and rotate the hip up and in; the fist travels up the centre line | Leaning too far forward, rising up on the toes and losing balance |

**Body shots:** add "b" (e.g. "1b" = jab to the body). **Bend your knees to lower your level**; don't just drop your hands and bend at the waist. The **lead hook to the body** (to the liver, on the opponent's right side) is one of the most effective punches in boxing.

**Universal punching rules:**
- **Exhale sharply** on every punch ("tss").
- **Return to guard** immediately after every punch. The hand comes back the same path it went out.
- Punch with the **first two knuckles** (index and middle).
- **Fist tight only at impact**, relaxed before and after (speed + power).
- **Power comes from the ground:** feet → legs → hips → shoulders → fist.
- **Chin down, eyes open** (don't look away or close your eyes).

### 1.7 Defence

"Hit and don't get hit" is the whole sport. Learn defence from Day 1.

| Defence | Against | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Block / high guard (cover)** | Hooks, straight punches | Bring gloves tight to the temples/forehead, elbows in, chin down. Absorb with the gloves and forearms. Best beginner defence |
| **Catch** | Jab, cross | Rear glove catches the incoming jab like catching a ball, close to your face (don't reach out) |
| **Parry** | Jab, cross | Small tap with your glove to push the punch off line (rear hand parries the jab across). Small movements only |
| **Slip** | Straight punches (jab, cross) | Rotate the torso slightly and **bend the knees**, moving your head just outside the punch (just a few centimetres). Slip outside the jab (to your right, orthodox) and inside or outside the cross |
| **Roll / bob and weave** | Hooks | Bend your knees and move your head in a **"U" shape** under the hook, from one side to the other. Keep your eyes on the opponent and hands up |
| **Pull / lean back** | Jab | Shift weight to the rear foot to take your head just out of range, then come back with a counter |
| **Shoulder roll** | Straight rear hand | Advanced (Philly shell). Learn later |
| **Footwork** | Everything | Step back or step off the line at an angle. Distance is the best defence |
| **Clinch** | Close-range pressure | Tie up the arms to stop punching. Learn with a coach |

**Counters:** slip the jab → throw a cross; roll under the hook → come up with a hook; catch the jab → throw a jab back.

### 1.8 Beginner combinations

| Combo | Why |
|---|---|
| **1-1** (double jab) | Range finding, pressure |
| **1-2** | The most important combination in boxing |
| **1-2-3** | Straight, straight, hook: sets up the hook |
| **1-1-2** | Rhythm change |
| **1-2-3-2** | Classic four-punch combo |
| **1-6-3-2** | Mix of long and short punches |
| **2-3-2** | Rear-hand start (after a slip) |
| **1-2-3b-3** | Body-head change (body hook then head hook) |
| **1-2 → slip → 2** | Defence into counter |
| **1-2 → roll → 3-2** | Roll under a hook, counter |
| **1-2 → pivot out** | Hit and leave at an angle |

**Rule:** every combination must **end with defence or movement**, never standing still in front of the opponent.

### 1.9 Training methods

| Method | What it builds | Beginner tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Shadowboxing** | Technique, footwork, rhythm, imagination | Use a mirror. Slow and perfect first, then faster. Visualise an opponent |
| **Heavy bag** | Power, stamina, punch correctness | Don't push the bag. Snap punches, move around it, don't stand flat |
| **Pad / mitt work** | Timing, accuracy, reaction, defence | Needs a knowledgeable partner or coach |
| **Double-end bag / reflex ball** | Timing, accuracy, head movement | Great for jab rhythm |
| **Speed bag** | Hand-eye coordination, shoulder endurance | Optional |
| **Skipping rope** | Footwork, rhythm, calf endurance, cardio | Build up to 3 x 3-minute rounds |
| **Partner drills (no head contact)** | Distance, defence reactions | Light, slow, controlled, body-only or on gloves |
| **Sparring** | Applying everything under pressure | **After 2 to 3 months of consistent training**, only with a coach, controlled intensity, proper gear |

### 1.10 Strength and conditioning

Boxing needs **aerobic endurance** (last all rounds), **anaerobic power** (explosive combos), strength, core stability, and a strong neck.

- **Cardio:** running 2 to 4 times a week (steady runs + intervals such as 30 seconds fast / 30 seconds slow), skipping, stairs.
- **Strength (2 to 3 times a week):** squats, lunges, deadlifts / hip hinges, push-ups, pull-ups / rows, overhead press, medicine ball throws (rotational power), glute bridges.
- **Core:** planks, side planks, dead bugs, Russian twists, hanging knee raises, bicycle crunches. **Core rotation = punch power.**
- **Neck:** start with gentle isometrics (press your hand against your head front, back and sides, 10 seconds each) or light band work. Leave neck bridges for coached settings. A strong neck may reduce head whip.
- **Mobility:** hips, thoracic spine (upper back rotation), shoulders, ankles.
- **Round-based work:** do conditioning in **3-minute rounds with 1-minute rest** so your body learns boxing rhythm.

### 1.11 Warm-up and cool-down

**Warm-up (8 to 10 min):** skipping or jogging 3 min → arm circles, shoulder rolls, hip circles, leg swings, torso twists → light shadowboxing 2 min.
**Cool-down (5 to 10 min):** walking, deep breathing, stretches for chest, shoulders, lats, hip flexors, hamstrings, calves, neck.

### 1.12 Nutrition, hydration and recovery

- **Protein:** active people doing strength work generally benefit from about **1.2 to 2.0 g per kg of body weight per day** (dal, paneer, eggs, curd, chicken, fish, soya, chana, milk).
- **Carbs** fuel training: rice, roti, oats, fruit, potatoes, millets.
- **Hydration:** drink before, during and after training. Add electrolytes in hot, humid weather (nimbu paani with salt and sugar, coconut water).
- **Sleep:** **7 to 9 hours**. Your body repairs and learns technique during sleep.
- **Rest days** prevent injury and burnout.
- **Never do extreme weight cuts** (dehydration, starving). They are dangerous, especially for the brain.

### 1.13 Safety and brain health

- **Concussion signs:** headache, dizziness, confusion, nausea, blurred vision, ringing ears, memory problems, sensitivity to light or noise, feeling "foggy." **Stop training immediately and see a doctor.** No contact until medically cleared, and follow a gradual return plan.
- **Headgear:** the evidence is **mixed**. Lab tests show approved headguards **roughly halve angular head acceleration** and greatly reduce cuts (McIntosh and Patton, *BJSM* 2015). Elite men's amateur boxing dropped headguards in 2013, and data showed fewer head-blow stoppages but many more cuts (Loosemore et al., 2017). No headgear can eliminate concussion risk, because the brain can still move inside the skull. **Beginners should always wear headgear for sparring**, and controlled sparring and good defence matter most.
- **Build defence first**, so you take fewer hits over your lifetime.
- **Never train with a bad partner** who goes hard on beginners. Tap gloves, agree on intensity.
- **Hands:** always wrap. Punch with correct alignment (wrist straight, first two knuckles).
- **Shoulders:** don't overextend punches; strengthen your rotator cuffs with light band work.
- **Stop** if you feel sharp pain in a joint.
- **Before you start:** answer a simple pre-exercise health questionnaire (the PAR-Q). If you answer yes to any question (chest pain, dizziness, heart or bone problems, medication for blood pressure), see a doctor first.
- **Heat:** in Indian summers, train early morning or evening, drink water and electrolytes, and stop at the first sign of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, confusion, no sweating).

### 1.14 Basic rules and fouls (for when you start partner work)

**Not allowed:** hitting below the belt, on the back of the head or neck (rabbit punch), on the kidneys/back, with an open glove, the inside of the glove, the wrist or the elbow; headbutting; holding and hitting; pushing; hitting after the bell or when an opponent is down; biting, spitting, wrestling or throwing.

**Scoring (amateur, simplified):** clean punches with the knuckle part of the glove to the target area, plus dominance, technique and tactics.

### 1.15 Boxing in India: where to train and how to progress

1. **Find a coach or club:** local boxing clubs and academies, **SAI (Sports Authority of India) centres** and **Khelo India** centres, school and college teams, police and army boxing units (for recruits), and many fitness gyms with boxing coaches. Check that the coach has a coaching diploma from the **Netaji Subhas National Institute of Sports (NSNIS, "NIS") Patiala**, part of SAI, or experience as a competitive boxer.
2. **Join competitions:** district boxing association → state championship → nationals. The official pathway runs through **state associations affiliated to the Boxing Federation of India (BFI)**. Ask your club to register you. Beware of unaffiliated bodies with similar names if you want an official competitive path.
3. **Age groups:** sub-junior, junior, youth and elite (senior), plus separate weight categories.
4. **Medical check** is needed before competing.
5. **Fitness boxing** (no competition) is available in many city studios.

### 1.16 The mental side

- Fear is normal. Everybody feels it. Controlled, gradual exposure builds confidence.
- **Breathe:** exhale on punches, breathe through your nose while moving when possible.
- **Stay relaxed:** tension wastes energy.
- **Keep a training diary:** what you worked on, what felt good, what to fix.
- **Watch and study:** watch rounds of skilled boxers (Vasyl Lomachenko for footwork, Floyd Mayweather for defence, Canelo Álvarez for head movement and counters, Mary Kom for aggression with technique).

## Part 2: 30-Day Plan

**Frequency:** 6 days a week, 45 to 75 minutes, but **heavy-bag work on only 3 to 4 of them** (make the other days shadowboxing, footwork and conditioning). **Day 7 each week is active rest.** If your knuckles, wrists or shoulders ache, drop a bag day.
**Every session starts** with the warm-up (1.11) and **ends** with the cool-down.
**Rounds:** start with **2-minute rounds / 1-minute rest**, move to **3-minute rounds** from Week 3.
**No sparring for these 30 days.** Partner drills are optional, light and controlled.

### Week 1: Stance, footwork, jab, cross, guard

| Day | Skill focus | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stance + guard + hand wrapping** | Learn to wrap (practise 3 times). Set your stance in the mirror 20 times. Balance test. 3 rounds skipping (or jogging in place). Core: 3 x 20 sec plank |
| 2 | **Footwork: step-drag in 4 directions** | 4 rounds footwork drills (square pattern, stance stays perfect). 3 rounds shadowboxing only moving and keeping your guard up. 3 x 10 bodyweight squats, 3 x 8 push-ups (knees OK) |
| 3 | **Jab (1)** | 3 rounds slow jab in the mirror (focus: snap, shoulder up, return). 3 rounds jab + step-drag forward and back. 3 rounds bag or air jabs. 3 rounds skipping |
| 4 | **Cross (2)** | 3 rounds slow cross (pivot rear foot, hip rotation). 3 rounds 1-2 shadowboxing. 3 rounds 1-2 on the bag. Core: dead bugs 3 x 10 each side |
| 5 | **Defence: high guard + catch + parry** | 2 rounds shadowbox 1-2 with a high guard after every combo. Catch and parry drill: a partner (both wearing gloves **and mouthguards**) jabs slowly at a **pad held beside your head** or stops short. No head contact. 3 rounds bag 1, 1-1, 1-2. 20 min easy run or brisk walk |
| 6 | **Put it together + conditioning** | 4 rounds shadowbox (1, 1-1, 1-2, move after each). 4 rounds bag. Circuit x 3: 10 squats, 10 push-ups, 20 mountain climbers, 30 sec plank |
| 7 | **Active rest** | 30 min walk, full-body stretching, review videos of the jab and cross |

### Week 2: Hooks, uppercuts, slipping

| Day | Skill focus | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | **Lead hook (3)** | 3 rounds slow hook (90-degree elbow, pivot the lead foot). 3 rounds 1-2-3 shadow. 3 rounds bag 3 and 1-2-3. Skipping 3 rounds |
| 9 | **Slip** (vs jab and cross) | Slip rope/string drill (tie a string at chin height, move under and along it) or slip a slip bag / a partner's slow jabs aimed at a held pad (gloves + mouthguards, no head contact). 3 rounds: slip left, slip right, counter with 2 or 1. 3 rounds bag. Core circuit |
| 10 | **Uppercuts (5 and 6)** | 3 rounds slow uppercuts (legs drive, hand stays up). 3 rounds shadow 1-2-5-2, 1-6-3-2. 3 rounds bag close range. Strength: 3 x 10 lunges each leg, 3 x 8 rows or pull-ups |
| 11 | **Rear hook (4) + footwork pivot** | 3 rounds pivot drills around a bag. 3 rounds 1-2-3-4. 3 rounds bag: combo then pivot out. 20 to 25 min run with 6 x 30 sec fast intervals |
| 12 | **Body shots** | 3 rounds: level change (bend the knees) with 1b, 2b, 3b. 3 rounds 1-2-3b-3. 3 rounds bag body-head. Core: side planks 3 x 20 sec each side, Russian twists |
| 13 | **Test day: all 6 punches** | 5 rounds shadow using all punches, slips and guard. 5 rounds bag (1 combo per round focus). Circuit x 4 |
| 14 | **Active rest** | Walk, stretch, foam roll. Record yourself shadowboxing 1 round and watch it for dropped hands and crossed feet |

### Week 3: Rolling, counters, 3-minute rounds

| Day | Skill focus | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | **Roll / bob and weave** under hooks | Rope drill: roll under the rope moving forward and backward. 3 rounds: roll then counter 3-2. 3 rounds bag. Skipping 3 x 3 min |
| 16 | **Counter-punching** | 3 rounds shadow: slip-2, catch-1, roll-3. 3 rounds bag with counters. Strength: squats, push-ups, rows, medicine ball twists (or a water jug) |
| 17 | **Pad work** (with a partner or coach if possible), else double-end or reflex ball | 4 rounds pad/mitt work (1-2, 1-2-3, slip-2, roll-3-2) or reflex ball. 3 rounds bag. 25 min run |
| 18 | **Angles: pivot, step off the line** | 3 rounds combos → pivot out. 3 rounds circle the bag (both directions) punching at angles. Core circuit |
| 19 | **Speed and rhythm** | 6 rounds of 30 sec fast straight punches / 30 sec slow technique. 3 rounds skipping (with double-unders attempts). Neck work (gentle), band shoulder work |
| 20 | **Power** | 5 rounds bag: 5 hard single punches with perfect form then 20 seconds of fast combos. Medicine ball rotational throws (or resistance-band punches; avoid punching with dumbbells, which stresses elbows and shoulders). Strength session |
| 21 | **Active rest** | Mobility, stretch, walk. Watch one full classic fight and note 3 techniques |

### Week 4: Combinations, flow and your first "fight simulation"

| Day | Skill focus | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | **Long combos** (4 to 6 punches) with defence at the end | 4 rounds shadow: 1-2-3-2 roll 3-2; 1-1-2-3b-3. 4 rounds bag. Skipping |
| 23 | **Inside fighting** (short hooks, uppercuts at close range) | 3 rounds close-range bag work (head on the bag's side, short shots). 3 rounds 5-6-3-4. Strength circuit |
| 24 | **Outside fighting** (jab and move) | 4 rounds: only 1, 1-1, 1-2 with constant movement. 3 rounds bag, in and out. 30 min run with intervals |
| 25 | **Defensive round work** | 3 rounds partner (light, body-only or glove targets) or shadow: defence only. 3 rounds: defence → counter. Core + neck |
| 26 | **Conditioning test** | 6 rounds x 3 min bag, 1 min rest, at bout intensity (twice the length of an amateur bout). Record how you felt each round |
| 27 | **Technique polish** | Film 2 rounds of shadowboxing. Fix your top 3 mistakes. 4 rounds focused drills on those mistakes |
| 28 | **Active rest** | Walk, stretch, light mobility |
| 29 | **Fight simulation** | 3 rounds x 3 min shadowboxing at full intensity (like an amateur bout) + 3 rounds x 3 min bag at full intensity, 1 min rest |
| 30 | **Assessment + next steps** | Re-do Day 1 stance/balance tests. Compare your videos from Day 14 and Day 27. Choose: join a club, find a coach, plan when to start controlled sparring (with a coach), set 90-day goals |

### Progress checklist
- [ ] I can wrap my hands correctly in under 3 minutes
- [ ] My stance stays balanced during all footwork
- [ ] I never cross my feet
- [ ] I throw all 6 punches with correct rotation
- [ ] My hands return to guard after every punch
- [ ] I can slip and roll smoothly
- [ ] I can do 3 counters automatically
- [ ] I can finish 6 x 3-minute bag rounds
- [ ] I can skip rope for 3 x 3 minutes without stopping
