# Martial Arts: Deep Research + 30-Day Plan

> A map of the world's martial arts (with special focus on India's own), what actually works for self-defence according to evidence, the myths, the law on self-defence in India, and a safe 30-day sampler plan. Boxing has its own guide: [02-boxing.md](02-boxing.md).
>
> **Safety:** learn throws, chokes and joint locks **only under a qualified instructor, on mats**. Chokes can cause unconsciousness in seconds.

---

## Part 1: Everything You Need to Know

### 1.1 What martial arts are

Martial arts are **systems of combat training**, practised for self-defence, sport, fitness, discipline, performance or spiritual growth. Most combine **techniques** (strikes, throws, holds, weapons), **training methods** (drills, forms, sparring) and a **philosophy or code**.

### 1.2 The main families

| Family | Arts | Range / focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Striking** | Boxing, Muay Thai (Thailand), kickboxing, karate (Okinawa/Japan), taekwondo (Korea), savate (France), Sanda/Sanshou (China), Lethwei (Myanmar), Musti Yuddha (India) | Punches, kicks, knees, elbows at stand-up range |
| **Grappling** | Wrestling (freestyle, Greco-Roman, folkstyle), **kushti / pehlwani** (India), judo (Japan), Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ), sambo (Russia), catch wrestling, mukna (Manipur) | Takedowns, throws, pins, chokes, joint locks |
| **Weapons** | Kendo and iaido (Japan), fencing (Europe), eskrima/arnis/kali (Philippines), **gatka** (Punjab), **silambam** (Tamil Nadu), **lathi khela** (Bengal), **Thang-Ta** (Manipur), **pari-khanda** (Bihar), archery | Sticks, swords, spears, shields |
| **Hybrid / complete** | **Kalaripayattu** (Kerala), MMA, Krav Maga (Israel), Jeet Kune Do (Bruce Lee's philosophy), Pencak Silat (Indonesia/Malaysia) | Striking + grappling + (sometimes) weapons |
| **Internal / meditative** | Tai chi (taijiquan), qigong, baguazhang, xingyiquan, aikido | Balance, flow, energy concepts, health |

**At the Olympics:**
- **Current events:** judo (since 1964), taekwondo (since 2000), boxing, wrestling (modern since 1896, ancient Greek origins) and fencing (since 1896).
- **Karate** appeared only at **Tokyo 2020**.

### 1.4 Major world arts at a glance

| Art | Origin | Core skill | Belt/rank system | Typical time to black belt* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Judo** | Japan (Kano Jigoro, 1882) | Throws, pins, chokes, armlocks | Kyu → dan (black belt) | ~4 to 8 years |
| **BJJ** | Brazil (from judo, Gracie family, 1920s onward) | Ground fighting and submissions | White → blue → purple → brown → black | **~8 to 15 years** |
| **Karate** | Okinawa → Japan | Strikes and kicks, kata (forms) | Kyu → dan | ~3 to 6 years (varies widely by school) |
| **Taekwondo** | Korea (1950s) | Kicks | Gup → dan | ~3 to 5 years |
| **Muay Thai** | Thailand | Punches, kicks, knees, elbows, clinch | Often none (fight record instead) | n/a |
| **Wrestling** | Worldwide, ancient | Takedowns, control | None (competition record) | n/a |
| **Aikido** | Japan (Ueshiba Morihei, 1920s to 30s) | Joint locks, throws using the attacker's energy | Kyu → dan | ~4 to 6 years |
| **Kung fu / wushu** | China | Hundreds of styles; modern wushu is performance (taolu) + Sanda | Varies | Varies |
| **Krav Maga** | Israel (Imi Lichtenfeld, 1930s to 40s) | Practical self-defence, weapon disarms, aggression | Levels | ~5+ years |
| **MMA** | Modern (UFC 1, **12 November 1993**) | Combination of striking, wrestling and BJJ | None | n/a |

*Estimates vary hugely by school, attendance and standards. A black belt means "competent in the basics," not "master."

### 1.5 What actually works: the evidence

- **UFC 1 (1993):** Royce Gracie, a smaller BJJ fighter, won the no-rules tournament by taking bigger opponents down and submitting them. This showed the world the value of grappling.
- **Modern MMA** has become a live laboratory: nearly every top fighter trains **wrestling, BJJ, Muay Thai and boxing**.
- **The key factor is "aliveness"** (a term popularised by coach Matt Thornton): training against a **fully resisting partner**. Arts that spar hard and often (boxing, Muay Thai, wrestling, judo, BJJ, MMA, kyokushin karate, sanda) build usable skill fastest. Arts that train mostly with cooperative partners or forms can build fitness and technique but are weaker under pressure unless the school adds sparring.
- **For health:** **tai chi** has strong evidence for reducing falls in older adults (meta-analyses show about a **24% lower fall risk**) and improves balance. Martial arts training generally improves fitness, coordination and confidence.

### 1.6 Real self-defence (what instructors who work with police and victims emphasise)

1. **Awareness:** most danger can be avoided by noticing it early (phone down, look around, trust your instincts).
2. **Avoidance:** don't go there, leave early, cross the road.
3. **De-escalation:** calm voice, open hands, apologise even if it's not your fault, give them a way out.
4. **Escape:** running away is the best technique. **Give up property** (phone, wallet); it is never worth your life.
5. **Fight only if you must**, then do so fully and escape at the first chance.
6. **Know that:**
   - Real attacks are fast, chaotic, often involve **multiple attackers or weapons**, and happen at close range.
   - **Knife "disarms" taught in many schools fail against a resisting attacker.** Escape is the answer to a knife.
   - Going to the ground on concrete with multiple attackers is dangerous. BJJ is excellent one-on-one but has limits in street chaos.
7. **Emergency number in India: 112.** Women's helpline 181. Many state police forces run free women's self-defence training.

### 1.7 Self-defence and the law in India

- The **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**, in force from **1 July 2024**, replaced the IPC. The **right of private defence** is in **sections 34 to 44** (old IPC sections 96 to 106).
- You may defend **your body, another person's body, and property** against unlawful harm.
- **Force must be reasonable and necessary** for the threat. You cannot keep hitting once the threat has ended, and there's no right of private defence when there's time to get help from the authorities.
- **Causing death** is justified only in serious cases listed in the law, such as a reasonable fear of death or grievous hurt, rape, kidnapping, or acid attack.
- **Carrying weapons:** the Arms Act and state laws restrict swords, knives and other weapons in public. Training weapons at an akhara are fine; carrying them in public may not be.
- If you ever use force: call 112, report it, and get legal advice.

### 1.8 Myths, legends and unproven claims

| Claim | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| **"Bodhidharma, an Indian monk, brought kung fu to the Shaolin Temple"** | **Not supported by historical records.** The earliest link appears centuries later. The *Yijin Jing* (a manual attributed to Bodhidharma) was written around **1624** by a Daoist and falsely attributed to him, as shown by historians Tang Hao, Xu Zhen and Matsuda Ryuchi. India–China martial exchange may have happened, but this story is legend |
| **"No-touch knockouts" using qi/ki** | **Fraud.** In 2006, "no-touch" master **Yanagi Ryuken** fought an MMA fighter and was beaten immediately. Such demonstrations work only on compliant students (suggestion and group pressure) |
| **"Death touch" (dim mak) / delayed-death strikes** | No evidence. Real vulnerable points (throat, eyes, groin, knee, back of head) are simply anatomy |
| **Marma points (kalaripayattu)** | Traditional knowledge of 107 vulnerable points. Many correspond to real vulnerable areas (joints, major vessels, nerves), but claims of mystical energy effects are unproven |
| **"Size doesn't matter"** | It does. That's why combat sports have **weight classes**. Technique narrows the gap; it doesn't erase it |
| **"Ninjas" as mystical assassins** | Historical shinobi existed as spies and irregulars in feudal Japan; most of the popular image comes from 20th-century fiction and films |
| **"Bushido" as an ancient samurai code** | The idea was systematised mainly in the Edo period and popularised by Nitobe Inazō's 1899 book *Bushido: The Soul of Japan* |
| **"My art is too deadly for sparring"** | A red flag: skills never tested under resistance usually fail when needed |

### 1.9 Choosing a school (checklist)

**Good signs:**
- Regular **live sparring or rolling** (after a few weeks of basics)
- An instructor with a **verifiable** rank and lineage, or a competition record
- Students who compete
- A clean, matted space
- Free trial classes
- Clear pricing
- An emphasis on safety and ego control

**Red flags:**
- Guaranteed black belt in a fixed time ("black belt in 2 years, pay upfront")
- Long locked-in contracts
- "Secret" or "too deadly to spar" techniques
- A cult-like atmosphere
- Instructors who can't be verified
- Injuring beginners

**Cost in India (approximate):** ₹1,500 to ₹6,000/month in cities for BJJ, MMA, Muay Thai or judo; kalaripayattu and traditional akharas are often cheaper; SAI/Khelo India centres may be subsidised.

### 1.10 Philosophy and the mind

- **"Do" (道, the Way):** in judo, karate-do, aikido and kendo, the art is a path of self-improvement, not just fighting.
- **Zen and the "empty mind" (mushin):** act without overthinking.
- **Kalaripayattu** traditionally combines training with worship of the kalari's guardian deity and healing arts.
- **Respect:** bowing, cleaning the mat, and the idea that the stronger person protects, not bullies.
- **Ego control:** "tap early, tap often" in BJJ. Losing in training is how you learn.

### 1.11 Education paths
- **Competition and coaching:** national federations (for example the Judo Federation of India, Taekwondo Federation of India, Wrestling Federation of India, Indian Kalaripayattu Federation), **NIS coaching diplomas** (Patiala and regional centres), and **B.P.Ed / M.P.Ed** degrees.
- **Careers:** instructor, gym owner, MMA/combat sports coach, police and defence training, fitness trainer, stunt performer, physiotherapist for athletes.

## Part 2: 30-Day Plan (a "sampler" to find your art)

**Goal:** build a base, try 3 different arts, and choose one to commit to. Train 5 days a week, 45 to 60 minutes, and rest 2 days. Days marked **(class)** mean attend a **free trial class**; book them in Week 1.

### Week 1: Foundation, movement and research

| Day | Focus | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Research (1.2 to 1.5) | Shortlist schools within reach: 1 striking, 1 grappling, 1 Indian traditional or tai chi. Book trial classes for Weeks 2 and 3 |
| 2 | General fitness base | Warm-up, then 3 rounds: 10 squats, 10 push-ups, 10 lunges, 20-second plank, 30 jumping jacks |
| 3 | Mobility (kalaripayattu-inspired) | Leg swings (front, side), deep squats, hip openers, spinal rolls, animal walks (crawl, bear, crab): 20 minutes |
| 4 | Stance and footwork | Fighting stance and step-drag footwork from [02-boxing.md](02-boxing.md) section 1.5, 4 x 2-minute rounds |
| 5 | **Breakfalls (ukemi) on a soft surface** | Back breakfall from sitting then squatting (chin tucked, slap the mat with your arms). Side breakfall. Don't try from standing without a mat and coach |
| 6-7 | Rest | Watch UFC 1 highlights and a kalaripayattu demonstration. Note the differences |

### Week 2: Striking

| Day | Focus | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Punches | Jab, cross, hook: shadowboxing 4 x 2 minutes |
| 9 | Kicks | Teep (push kick) and roundhouse kick (pivot on the ball of the standing foot, turn the hip over), slowly, 3 x 10 each side |
| 10 | **(class)** Striking art trial: Muay Thai, kickboxing, karate or taekwondo | Note: did they spar? Was it safe? How were beginners treated? |
| 11 | Knees, elbows, combos | Shadow: jab-cross-roundhouse, jab-cross-knee. 4 rounds |
| 12 | Conditioning | 5 x 1-minute fast shadowboxing / 1-minute rest, then core work |
| 13-14 | Rest + reflection | Journal: what you liked and disliked about striking |

### Week 3: Grappling and traditional arts

| Day | Focus | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Ground movement solo drills | Shrimping (hip escape), bridging, technical stand-up, sit-outs: 4 x 2 minutes |
| 16 | **(class)** Grappling trial: BJJ, judo or wrestling | Tell the coach you're new. Learn a safe tap. Tap early |
| 17 | Grappling conditioning | Bear crawls, sprawls, burpees, neck and grip work (towel hangs) |
| 18 | **(class)** Traditional trial: kalaripayattu, silambam, gatka or tai chi | Note: movement quality, conditioning, and whether there's any applied practice |
| 19 | Balance and flexibility | Tai chi-style slow movements 15 minutes + stretching. Single-leg balance drills |
| 20-21 | Rest + reflection | Compare the 3 trials using the checklist in 1.9 |

### Week 4: Self-defence, decision and commitment

| Day | Focus | Session |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | Awareness and de-escalation (1.6) | Practise "fence" posture (hands up and open, calm voice) with a friend, role-playing verbal de-escalation |
| 23 | Escapes from grabs (with a partner, slowly) | Wrist-grab release toward the thumb, then step away. Breaking a bear hug by lowering your base. Always finish by **creating distance and leaving** |
| 24 | The law (1.7) | Read about BNS sections 34 to 44. Write in your own words what "reasonable force" means |
| 25 | **(class)** Second visit to your favourite school | See if you still like it on a normal day |
| 26 | Full session | 30 minutes: mobility, shadowboxing, solo ground drills, then conditioning |
| 27 | Myths (1.8) | Watch the Yanagi Ryuken video and read about the Bodhidharma legend. Note how to spot a fake master |
| 28 | Rest | |
| 29 | Test | Repeat the Day 2 circuit and time it. Compare with Day 2 |
| 30 | **Decide and commit** | Choose one art (or one striking + one grappling). Sign up month-to-month, not a long contract. Set a 6-month goal (e.g. first competition, first stripe/belt) |
