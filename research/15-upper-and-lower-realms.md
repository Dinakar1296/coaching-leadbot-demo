# Upper and Lower Realms: Deep Research + 30-Day Plan

> How the world's religions and traditions describe **heavens, hells and other planes of existence**: Hindu lokas, Buddhist realms, Jain cosmology, Sikh khands, Abrahamic heaven and hell, and Greek, Norse, Chinese, Egyptian and Mesoamerican underworlds. It also covers what science can and cannot say (near-death experience research), and the **physical** "upper and lower realms" we can actually measure: the atmosphere, space, and the deep Earth and ocean.
>
> **These realms are matters of faith and scripture.** They are described here as each tradition teaches them, respectfully, without claiming they are physically proven or disproven.
>
> **Verification note:** this session's web-search allowance ran out before this guide was written. Details come from standard scriptures and reference works (knowledge up to mid-2026) and were **not re-checked live**. Traditions have many schools, and details vary between texts.

## Part 1: Everything You Need to Know

### 1.1 Hindu cosmology: the 14 lokas

In the Puranas (especially the ***Bhagavata Purana*, Book 5**, and the ***Vishnu Purana***), the universe (*brahmanda*, the "cosmic egg") contains **14 worlds (lokas)**: 7 upper and 7 lower, with Earth among the upper worlds.

**The seven upper worlds (vyahrtis), from lowest to highest:**

| # | Loka | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bhu-loka** | Earth, the human world |
| 2 | **Bhuvar-loka** | The atmosphere/sky between Earth and heaven; realm of sages and spirits |
| 3 | **Svar-loka (Svarga)** | Heaven of **Indra** and the devas; enjoyed as a reward for good karma, but temporary |
| 4 | **Mahar-loka** | Realm of great sages (e.g. Bhrigu), which survives the partial dissolution |
| 5 | **Jana-loka** | Realm of Brahma's mind-born sons (e.g. the four Kumaras) |
| 6 | **Tapa-loka** | Realm of great ascetics |
| 7 | **Satya-loka (Brahma-loka)** | Abode of **Brahma**, the creator; the highest world within the universe |

The first three are named in the **Gayatri mantra**'s opening: "**Om bhur bhuvah svah**" (Earth, atmosphere, heaven).

**The seven lower worlds (the talas or patalas), from highest to lowest:**

| # | Tala | Inhabitants (per the *Bhagavata Purana*) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Atala** | Ruled by Bala, a son of Maya |
| 2 | **Vitala** | A form of Shiva (Hara-Bhava) with his attendants |
| 3 | **Sutala** | **King Bali**, the generous asura king, placed here by Vamana (Vishnu's dwarf avatar) and protected by Vishnu |
| 4 | **Talatala** | **Maya (Mayasura)**, the great architect of the asuras |
| 5 | **Mahatala** | Many-headed serpents (the *Kadraveya* nagas) |
| 6 | **Rasatala** | Daityas and danavas (enemies of the devas) |
| 7 | **Patala (Nagaloka)** | The **nagas** (serpent beings) led by Vasuki, with jewel-lit palaces |

The lower worlds are described as **beautiful and luxurious**, lit by the jewels on the serpents' hoods, not as hells. **Below Patala** lies the great serpent **Shesha (Ananta)**, who supports the worlds.

**Naraka (hells)** are separate: places of punishment under **Yama**, the god of death and justice. The *Bhagavata Purana* lists **28 narakas** (e.g. Tamisra, Raurava, Kumbhipaka). The *Garuda Purana* describes the soul's journey after death. **Stays in both heaven and hell are temporary**; the soul is reborn according to karma.

**Beyond the 14 worlds:** the eternal abodes of the divine, which lie beyond the cycle of rebirth:
- **Vaikuntha** (Vishnu)
- **Kailasa** (Shiva)
- **Goloka** (Krishna, in Vaishnava traditions)
- **Manidvipa** (the Goddess, in Shakta traditions)

**Liberation (moksha)** means leaving the cycle of rebirth altogether.

**Puranic geography:**
- **Mount Meru** is the axis at the centre.
- Around it lie **seven concentric island-continents (dvipas)** separated by **seven oceans** (salt, sugarcane juice, wine, ghee, curd, milk, fresh water).
- The innermost is **Jambudvipa**, which contains **Bharatavarsha** (India).

**Cosmic time:**
- **Four yugas** (Satya, Treta, Dvapara, Kali) together make a **Mahayuga** of 4.32 million years.
- **1,000 Mahayugas = one day of Brahma (a kalpa) = 4.32 billion years**, followed by a night of dissolution (*pralaya*).
- Creation and dissolution repeat in endless cycles.
- Carl Sagan noted in *Cosmos* (1980) that Hindu cosmology is the only ancient tradition whose timescales match the billions of years of modern cosmology (as a coincidence of scale, not as scientific proof).

**Other Hindu schemes:**
- **Pancha kosha** (five sheaths of the self) in the *Taittiriya Upanishad*.
- **Chakras** in tantra and yoga as inner "levels" in the subtle body.
- Some teachers interpret the lokas as **states of consciousness** rather than physical places.

### 1.2 Buddhism: the realms of rebirth

**Six realms (the "Wheel of Life," *bhavachakra*):**

| Realm | Beings | Cause (traditional) |
|---|---|---|
| **Deva** (gods) | Long-lived, blissful, but still mortal | Generosity, virtue, meditation (though pride can follow) |
| **Asura** (demigods / titans) | Powerful, jealous, constantly fighting | Envy, rivalry |
| **Manushya** (humans) | Mixed happiness and suffering | **The best birth for reaching enlightenment** |
| **Tiryak** (animals) | Driven by instinct and fear | Ignorance |
| **Preta** (hungry ghosts) | Huge bellies and tiny mouths, never satisfied | Greed, craving |
| **Naraka** (hells) | Hot and cold hells of intense but temporary suffering | Hatred, violence |

**Three realms (*tridhatu*):**
1. **Kama-dhatu** (desire realm), which includes the six realms above
2. **Rupa-dhatu** (form realm): 16 to 18 heavens reached through meditative absorption (*jhana*)
3. **Arupa-dhatu** (formless realm): 4 formless states

Theravada lists **31 planes of existence** in total.

- **All are part of samsara.** **Nirvana** is not a realm but the end of rebirth.
- **Mahayana "Pure Lands":** e.g. **Sukhavati**, the Western Paradise of **Amitabha Buddha**, a realm ideal for attaining enlightenment (central to Pure Land Buddhism in East Asia).
- **Psychological reading:** many teachers (especially in Tibetan and Zen traditions) also interpret the six realms as **mental states** that people cycle through daily: anger (hell), craving (hungry ghost), dullness (animal), jealousy (asura), pride (god).
- **See it:** the **Bhavachakra painting** is at the entrance of many Tibetan monasteries (e.g. in Sikkim, Ladakh and Himachal Pradesh), and in the **Ajanta caves** (Cave 17 has a Wheel of Life painting).

### 1.3 Jain cosmology

- The universe (**loka**) is **uncreated and eternal**, shaped like a **standing person with hands on hips** (or like stacked drums), surrounded by infinite empty space (**aloka**).
- **Three worlds:**
  - **Urdhva-loka** (upper world): the heavens of the gods. In the Shvetambara tradition, **12 kalpa heavens** (Digambaras count 16), then the **9 graiveyaka** heavens, then the **5 anuttara** heavens.
  - **Madhya-loka** (middle world): innumerable concentric continents and oceans with **Jambudvipa** and **Mount Meru** at the centre; humans and animals live here.
  - **Adho-loka** (lower world): **7 hells** (*narakas*), each darker and more painful: Ratnaprabha, Sharkaraprabha, Valukaprabha, Pankaprabha, Dhumaprabha, Tamahprabha and Mahatamahprabha.
- **At the very top, Siddhashila:** the crescent-shaped abode of **liberated souls (siddhas)**, who dwell in infinite knowledge and bliss forever.
- **See it:** beautiful Jain cosmological cloth paintings (*loka purusha*, *adhai dvipa*) are held in museums in Ahmedabad (e.g. the L.D. Institute of Indology) and around the world.

### 1.4 Sikhism

- Guru Nanak rejected the idea of a fixed number of worlds. In the **Japji Sahib** (Pauri 22): *"Paataalaa paataal lakh aagaasaa aagaas"*: **"There are hundreds of thousands of nether worlds, and hundreds of thousands of skies"** above. Creation is beyond counting; only God knows its extent.
- **The five khands (realms of spiritual progress), Japji Sahib Pauris 34 to 37:**
  1. **Dharam Khand:** the realm of duty and righteous action
  2. **Gian Khand:** the realm of knowledge
  3. **Saram Khand:** the realm of spiritual effort and beauty
  4. **Karam Khand:** the realm of grace
  5. **Sach Khand:** the realm of truth, where the Formless One dwells
- These are usually understood as **stages of the soul's inner journey**, not physical places. Sikhism emphasises living righteously now rather than seeking heaven or fearing hell.

### 1.5 Abrahamic traditions

| Tradition | Upper realm | Lower realm | In between |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Judaism** | **Olam Ha-Ba** (the World to Come), Gan Eden (paradise) | **Sheol** (early Hebrew Bible: a shadowy realm of all the dead), later **Gehinnom** (a place of purification, usually temporary) | Kabbalah describes four worlds and ten sefirot |
| **Christianity** | **Heaven** (union with God). Paul mentions "the third heaven" (2 Corinthians 12) | **Hell** (eternal separation from God) | **Purgatory** (Catholic teaching: purification before heaven). Limbo (a historical theological idea, not official doctrine) |
| **Islam** | **Jannah** (paradise), often described with **7 or 8 levels**; the highest is **Firdaws** | **Jahannam** (hell) with **7 gates/levels** | **Barzakh** (the barrier between death and resurrection). The **Mi'raj** (Night Journey) tells of the Prophet ascending through **seven heavens** |

**Dante's *Divine Comedy*** (early 14th century), a poem rather than scripture, shaped European imagination:
- ***Inferno***: **9 circles of hell**
- ***Purgatorio***: a mountain with **7 terraces** (one per deadly sin)
- ***Paradiso***: **9 celestial spheres + the Empyrean**

### 1.6 Other world traditions

| Tradition | Realms |
|---|---|
| **Ancient Egypt** | **Duat** (the underworld). The dead face the **weighing of the heart** against the feather of **Ma'at** (truth); the pure reach the **Field of Reeds** (*Aaru*). Described in the *Book of the Dead* |
| **Ancient Greece** | **Mount Olympus** (the gods); **Hades** (the underworld, ruled by the god Hades), with the **Elysian Fields** (the blessed), the **Asphodel Meadows** (ordinary souls), and **Tartarus** (a deep abyss of punishment). The rivers Styx and Lethe (forgetting) |
| **Norse** | **Nine worlds** on the world tree **Yggdrasil**: **Asgard** (the gods), **Midgard** (humans), Jotunheim (giants), Alfheim, Vanaheim, Svartalfheim, Niflheim, Muspelheim, and **Helheim** (the realm of Hel). **Valhalla** in Asgard for warriors chosen by Odin |
| **Chinese** | **Tian** (Heaven). Daoist hierarchies of heavens (e.g. the **Thirty-Six Heavens**). **Diyu** (hell) with its judges (the **Ten Kings**) and, in popular tradition, **18 levels** |
| **Aztec (Mexica)** | **13 heavens** and **9 levels of Mictlan** (the underworld), which the dead crossed over four years |
| **Maya** | **Xibalba**, the underworld of the *Popol Vuh*, challenged by the Hero Twins |
| **Shamanic traditions** (Siberia, the Americas, parts of tribal India) | A common **three-world model:** **upper world**, **middle world** (ours), **lower world**, connected by a world tree or axis. The shaman travels between them in trance |
| **Theosophy** (19th century onward) | **Seven planes:** physical, astral, mental, buddhic, atmic, monadic, divine. Popularised the "**astral plane**" in the West |

**Recurring patterns** (studied in comparative religion):
- A vertical axis (a mountain or tree)
- Upper = divine, light, reward
- Lower = death, darkness, the ancestors
- Multiple levels
- Judgement after death
- In Indian religions, **temporary** heavens and hells within a cycle of rebirth, versus **final** destinations in the Abrahamic faiths

### 1.7 What science can and cannot say

- **Science cannot test** whether non-physical realms exist; they are, by their nature, outside physical measurement. **No scientific instrument has detected them**, and none has disproved them. They remain matters of faith, philosophy and personal experience.
- **Near-death experiences (NDEs)** are **real experiences** reported by many people who survive life-threatening events: a tunnel, light, peace, meeting deceased relatives, a life review, a feeling of leaving the body.
  - A large study in *The Lancet* (**van Lommel et al., 2001**) found about **18%** of cardiac-arrest survivors in Dutch hospitals reported an NDE.
  - The **AWARE studies** (Parnia et al., 2014, and AWARE II, published 2023) placed hidden images in resuscitation rooms to test out-of-body claims. **No patient identified a hidden image**, though some reported vivid awareness during CPR, and brain activity (gamma, delta) was detected up to an hour into CPR in some patients.
  - **Proposed brain-based explanations:** oxygen shortage, carbon dioxide build-up, REM-sleep intrusion, endorphins, temporal-lobe activity.
  - **The interpretation is debated.** Some researchers argue these don't fully explain the clarity of the experiences.
- **Reincarnation research:** Ian Stevenson (University of Virginia) collected thousands of cases of children claiming past-life memories, many from **India**, Sri Lanka and Lebanon. Supporters find them compelling; critics point to cultural influence, leading questions and memory contamination. **Not accepted as scientific proof.**
- **Psychology:** beliefs about heaven and hell influence behaviour and wellbeing. Research on religious coping shows both comfort (hope, meaning) and distress (fear of punishment) depending on the person.

### 1.8 The physical upper and lower realms (measured)

| "Realm" | Where | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Troposphere** | 0 to ~8 to 18 km | Weather; Everest's summit is 8,849 m (2020 China-Nepal survey) |
| **Stratosphere** | ~12 to 50 km | **Ozone layer**; jets fly in its lower part |
| **Mesosphere** | ~50 to 85 km | Meteors burn up here |
| **Kármán line** | ~100 km | A common boundary of space |
| **Thermosphere** | ~85 to 600 km | Auroras; the ISS orbits at ~400 km; temperatures can exceed 1,000°C but it would feel cold (the air is too thin) |
| **Exosphere** | ~600 to 10,000 km | Fades into space |
| **Beyond** | | Geostationary orbit at 35,786 km; the Moon at ~384,400 km; **Aditya-L1** (ISRO's solar observatory) at the Sun-Earth L1 point, ~1.5 million km away; the observable universe is ~93 billion light-years across |
| **Deepest ocean** | Challenger Deep, Mariana Trench | ~10,935 m. India's **Samudrayaan** aims to send 3 people to 6,000 m in the **Matsya 6000** submersible |
| **Deepest hole** | Kola Superdeep Borehole | 12,262 m |
| **Earth's centre** | 6,371 km down | Solid iron-nickel inner core at ~5,000 to 6,000°C (see [11-planets-structure.md](11-planets-structure.md)) |
| **Deep life** | Kilometres below ground | Microbes live in rock several km deep: a real "lower realm" of life |

### 1.9 Myths and misunderstandings

| Claim | Clarification |
|---|---|
| "Patala = America / the other side of the Earth" | A modern speculative reinterpretation; the Puranas describe Patala as below the Earth, not across it |
| "Hindu lower worlds are hells" | No. The talas are luxurious realms; the narakas are separate |
| "Buddhist heavens are the goal" | No. They're temporary; the goal is nirvana |
| "Scientists have proven heaven or hell exists / doesn't exist" | Neither. Science can't test non-physical realms |
| "Astral projection lets you visit other realms" | Many people report such experiences; controlled tests (e.g. identifying hidden targets) have not confirmed them |
| "All religions describe the same realms" | They share patterns but differ deeply in details and meaning |

## Part 2: 30-Day Plan

About **40 to 60 minutes a day**. Keep a comparison chart that you fill in as you go: tradition | upper realms | lower realms | what decides where you go | is it permanent?

### Week 1: Hindu cosmology

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Overview of the 14 lokas (1.1) | Draw the 14 worlds as a vertical diagram |
| 2 | *Bhagavata Purana* Book 5, chapter 24 (the lower worlds) | Read a translation; add details to your diagram |
| 3 | Svarga and the upper lokas | Chant or listen to the Gayatri mantra; note "bhur bhuvah svah" |
| 4 | Naraka and Yama; the *Katha Upanishad*'s Nachiketa | Compare the fear-based and the wisdom-based views of death |
| 5 | Mount Meru, the dvipas and oceans | Draw the Puranic world map (concentric rings) |
| 6 | Yugas and kalpas | Calculate: how many human years are in one day of Brahma? (4.32 billion) |
| 7 | **Review** | Vaikuntha, Kailasa, moksha: write how "beyond the lokas" differs from "the highest loka" |

### Week 2: Buddhist, Jain and Sikh views

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | The six realms and the Wheel of Life (1.2) | Study a Bhavachakra image; label each section |
| 9 | Three realms and 31 planes | Make a table of the 31 planes |
| 10 | Realms as mental states | Journal: which "realm" did you visit today (anger, craving, pride)? |
| 11 | Pure Land Buddhism | Read about Amitabha and Sukhavati |
| 12 | Jain cosmology (1.3) | Draw the loka purusha shape with its three worlds and Siddhashila |
| 13 | Japji Sahib Pauri 22 and the five khands (1.4) | Read or listen to the Japji Sahib (about 20 minutes) |
