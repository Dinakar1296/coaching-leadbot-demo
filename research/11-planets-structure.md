## Part 1: Everything You Need to Know

### 1.1 What is a planet?

The **International Astronomical Union (IAU), 2006:** a planet is a body that
1. orbits the Sun,
2. is massive enough for gravity to pull it **round** (hydrostatic equilibrium), and
3. has **cleared its orbital neighbourhood** of other objects.

**Pluto** fails the third test, so it became a **dwarf planet**, along with Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake. Some planetary scientists still dispute the definition.

### 1.2 How planets form (the nebular model)

1. **~4.57 billion years ago**, a cloud of gas and dust collapses. The oldest solid grains in meteorites (calcium-aluminium-rich inclusions) date to about **4.567 billion years**.
2. The cloud flattens into a spinning **protoplanetary disk** around the young Sun. (We now photograph such disks around other stars, e.g. HL Tauri with the ALMA telescope, 2014.)
3. Dust sticks together → pebbles → **planetesimals** (km-sized) → **protoplanets**.
4. **The frost line** (about 3 to 5 times Earth's distance from the Sun): inside it, only rock and metal can condense, giving **small rocky planets**; outside it, ice also condenses, so cores grow big enough to grab gas, giving **giant planets**.
5. **Differentiation:** young planets are hot and molten; heavy iron sinks to form a **core** and lighter rock floats up to form the **mantle and crust**.
6. **Migration:** giant planets move after forming (the "Nice model" and "Grand Tack" hypotheses), scattering smaller bodies and shaping the asteroid belt and Kuiper belt.
7. **The Moon** most likely formed when a Mars-sized body (called **Theia**) hit the young Earth about 4.5 billion years ago (the giant impact hypothesis).

### 1.3 Types of planets

| Type | Examples | Make-up |
|---|---|---|
| **Terrestrial (rocky)** | Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars | Iron core, rocky mantle, thin crust |
| **Gas giants** | Jupiter, Saturn | Mostly hydrogen and helium; deep inside, hydrogen becomes **metallic** (conducts electricity) |
| **Ice giants** | Uranus, Neptune | Water, ammonia and methane "ices" (hot, dense fluids) over a rocky core, with a hydrogen-helium envelope |
| **Dwarf planets** | Pluto, Ceres, Eris | Rock and ice |
| **Exoplanet types** (no Solar System example) | **Super-Earths**, **mini-Neptunes** (the most common planets in the galaxy), **hot Jupiters**, **lava worlds**, possible **water worlds** | Varies |

### 1.4 Inside Earth: the best-known planet

| Layer | Depth (km) | State | Main material | Temperature (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Crust** | 0 to 5 to 10 (ocean), 30 to 50 (continent), up to ~70 (under the Himalaya and Tibet) | Solid | Oceanic: basalt; continental: granite-type rocks | 0 to ~500°C |
| **Moho** (boundary) | Base of the crust | | Found by Andrija Mohorovičić, 1909 | |
| **Upper mantle** (including the asthenosphere) | to ~410 | Solid but slowly flowing | Peridotite (olivine-rich rock) | ~500 to 1,500°C |
| **Transition zone** | 410 to 660 | Solid | High-pressure forms of olivine; may hold lots of water in minerals | |
| **Lower mantle** | 660 to ~2,890 | Solid | Bridgmanite (Earth's most abundant mineral) | up to ~3,500°C |
| **Outer core** | ~2,890 to ~5,150 | **Liquid** | Iron-nickel + light elements | ~4,000 to 5,000°C |
| **Inner core** | ~5,150 to 6,371 (centre) | **Solid** (because of enormous pressure) | Iron-nickel; radius ~1,220 km | ~5,000 to 6,000°C, similar to the Sun's surface |

**How we know (nobody has drilled past the crust):**
- **Seismology.** Earthquake waves bend, speed up, slow down and reflect at boundaries.
  - **S-waves (shear waves) can't travel through liquid.** Their "shadow zone" showed the outer core is liquid (Richard Oldham 1906, Beno Gutenberg 1914).
  - **Inge Lehmann (1936)** discovered the solid inner core.
- **The deepest hole ever drilled**, the Kola Superdeep Borehole (Russia), reached **12,262 m in 1989**, only about 0.2% of the way to the centre.
- **Meteorites** (samples of other planets' cores and mantles), **lab experiments** at extreme pressures (diamond anvil cells), and **Earth's gravity and magnetic field**.
- **Recent research (debated):** studies in 2023 and 2024 suggested the inner core's rotation relative to the mantle **slowed and may have reversed** slightly, and that there may be a distinct "**innermost inner core**." These are active research areas, not settled facts.

**Why the structure matters:**
- **Magnetic field:** convection of liquid iron in the outer core acts as a **geodynamo**. The field shields life from solar wind and cosmic rays, and it flips polarity every few hundred thousand years on average (the last full reversal was about 780,000 years ago).
- **Plate tectonics:** the crust and upper mantle (the lithosphere) are broken into plates that move a few cm per year, driven by mantle convection. Alfred Wegener proposed continental drift in 1912; plate tectonics was accepted in the 1960s.
- **India's journey:** the Indian plate broke from Gondwana, moved north unusually fast (up to ~15 to 20 cm/year at times), and **collided with Asia about 50 million years ago**, pushing up the **Himalaya and Tibet**. It still moves north at about **4 to 5 cm per year**, which is why the Himalaya has large earthquakes (e.g. Nepal 2015). The **Deccan Traps** in Maharashtra are huge lava flows from about 66 million years ago.

### 1.5 The other planets, layer by layer

| Planet | Structure highlights | How we know |
|---|---|---|
| **Mercury** | **Enormous iron core** (~85% of its radius); thin mantle; shrinking (wrinkle ridges) | MESSENGER (NASA, 2011 to 2015); BepiColombo (ESA/JAXA) arriving 2026 |
| **Venus** | Similar in size to Earth, but a crushing **~92 bar** CO₂ atmosphere, surface **~465°C** (hotter than Mercury, from a runaway greenhouse effect), sulphuric acid clouds, very slow backward rotation (a day is longer than its year), no plate tectonics | Soviet Venera landers, Magellan radar. **ISRO's Venus Orbiter Mission (Shukrayaan)** was approved in 2024 for a planned launch around 2028 |
| **Earth** | See 1.4 | |
| **Moon** | Crust ~30 to 50 km, mantle, small iron core (radius ~300 to 350 km, partly molten) | Apollo seismometers (1969 to 1977), GRAIL. **Chandrayaan-1 (2008)** helped confirm water/hydroxyl on the surface. **Chandrayaan-3 (landed 23 August 2023)**, the first landing near the lunar **south pole**: the Pragyan rover confirmed **sulphur** and measured other elements; the lander measured the soil's temperature profile |
| **Mars** | Crust roughly 24 to 72 km thick; a **large liquid iron core** (radius estimates from about 1,650 to 1,830 km); the tallest volcano, **Olympus Mons** (~22 km high); no global magnetic field today | **NASA InSight** seismometer (2018 to 2022) measured "marsquakes." **ISRO's Mangalyaan** (Mars Orbiter Mission, 2013 to 2022) made India the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit, and the first nation to succeed on its first attempt |
| **Jupiter** | ~318 Earth masses; mostly hydrogen and helium; metallic hydrogen layer; Juno found a **"dilute" or "fuzzy" core** mixed into the surrounding layers rather than a sharp small core; Great Red Spot (a storm, shrinking); ~95+ known moons (approx.) | Galileo probe (1995), **Juno** (2016 onward) |
| **Saturn** | Less dense than water (average ~0.69 g/cm³); rings of ice (the rings' age, young or old, is **debated**); the most known moons of any planet (**~270+**, approx.); Titan has a thick atmosphere and methane lakes; Enceladus sprays water from an under-ice ocean | **Cassini** (2004 to 2017) |
| **Uranus** | Ice giant tilted ~98° (it rolls on its side); coldest planetary atmosphere (~−224°C) | Voyager 2 (1986) only. A flagship Uranus mission is a top recommended priority |
| **Neptune** | Ice giant; **fastest winds** in the Solar System (~2,000 km/h); moon Triton orbits backwards (probably a captured Kuiper belt object) | Voyager 2 (1989) |
| **Pluto** | Rock-ice dwarf with nitrogen-ice glaciers (Sputnik Planitia) and possibly a subsurface ocean | **New Horizons** (2015) |

**"Diamond rain"** inside Uranus and Neptune (carbon from methane squeezed into diamond) is supported by laser experiments but is **still a hypothesis** for the real planets.

### 1.6 The architecture of planetary systems

- **Our Solar System's layout:** four small rocky planets inside, the **asteroid belt** (Mars to Jupiter), four giants outside, then the **Kuiper belt** (Pluto and icy bodies, 30 to 50 AU), the scattered disk, and the hypothetical **Oort cloud** (up to ~100,000 AU, the source of long-period comets).
  - **1 AU** = Earth–Sun distance ≈ 150 million km.
  - Light takes about **8 minutes 20 seconds** from the Sun to Earth, and about 4 hours to Neptune.
- **Orbits follow Kepler's laws (1609 to 1619):**
  - Orbits are ellipses.
  - Planets sweep equal areas in equal times.
  - T² ∝ a³ (orbital period squared is proportional to distance cubed).
  - Newton explained why in 1687 (gravity).
- **Resonances:** simple ratios of orbital periods, e.g. Neptune and Pluto 3:2; Jupiter's moons Io, Europa and Ganymede 1:2:4. Gaps in the asteroid belt (Kirkwood gaps) come from resonances with Jupiter.
- **The "Titius-Bode law"** (a number pattern for planet distances) is **not a physical law**; Neptune doesn't fit it. It's most likely a coincidence plus a general tendency for planets to be spaced roughly geometrically.
- **Other systems are very different:**
  - Over **6,000 exoplanets** are confirmed (approx., 2025).
  - Many systems have **tightly packed super-Earths** closer to their star than Mercury is to the Sun, and planets in the same system often look alike ("peas in a pod").
  - **TRAPPIST-1** has seven Earth-sized planets.
  - **Hot Jupiters** migrated inward.
  - Our system, with giants far out and nothing inside Mercury's orbit, may be somewhat unusual.
- **How exoplanets are found:**
  - **Transit:** a star dims as a planet passes (Kepler, TESS).
  - **Radial velocity:** the star wobbles.
  - **Direct imaging.**
  - **Microlensing.**
  - **JWST** analyses atmospheres by starlight passing through them.

### 1.7 Building on other planets (human "architecture")

| Challenge | Moon | Mars | Solutions being studied |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radiation** | No atmosphere or magnetic field | Thin atmosphere, no global field | Bury habitats under **regolith (soil) several metres deep**, use **lava tubes** (natural caves), water or polyethylene shielding |
| **Pressure / air** | Vacuum | ~0.6% of Earth's pressure, mostly CO₂ | Pressurised modules, inflatable habitats |
| **Temperature** | ~+120°C day to −130°C night (colder in polar craters) | ~−60°C average | Insulation, buried habitats |
| **Dust** | Sharp, clingy lunar dust damages seals and lungs | Fine dust, global dust storms | Airlocks, dust-repelling coatings |
| **Materials** | Regolith, ice at the poles | Regolith, subsurface ice | **ISRU** (in-situ resource utilisation): 3D-print with regolith ("lunarcrete"), extract oxygen from regolith, mine ice for water and rocket fuel. NASA's MOXIE made oxygen from Martian CO₂ (2021 to 2023) |
| **Gravity** | ~1/6 of Earth's | ~3/8 of Earth's | Health effects of long stays are unknown |

**India's plans:** Gaganyaan (human spaceflight), **Bharatiya Antariksh Station** (India's space station, targeted by 2035), and an Indian crewed Moon landing targeted around 2040. Indian institutes (e.g. IISc) have researched **"space bricks"** made from lunar/Martian soil simulant using bacteria.

### 1.8 Traditional views: Indian astronomy and astrology

- **Indian astronomy (jyotisha as science):**
  - **Aryabhata** (*Aryabhatiya*, 499 CE) proposed that **Earth rotates on its axis** and gave accurate values for planetary periods and π (3.1416).
  - The *Surya Siddhanta*, Varahamihira and Brahmagupta computed eclipses.
  - These were genuine scientific achievements.
- **Astrology (phalita jyotisha):**
  - It uses the **navagraha** (Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, plus **Rahu and Ketu**, which are the Moon's orbital nodes, not physical bodies) to predict life events.
  - It is culturally important, but **scientific tests have not supported it**. Shawn Carlson's double-blind test (*Nature*, 1985) found astrologers matched personality profiles to birth charts no better than chance.
  - The gravitational pull of distant planets on a newborn is far smaller than that of the doctor delivering the baby.
- **Myths:**
  - **"Planetary alignments cause disasters":** they have no measurable effect on Earth.
  - **"Nibiru / Planet X will hit Earth":** there's no such object. (A distant "Planet Nine" is a real scientific **hypothesis** to explain some Kuiper belt orbits, but it hasn't been found.)

### 1.9 Education paths
- **Planetary science / astrophysics:** B.Sc./M.Sc. Physics or Geology, then a Ph.D.
  - India: **PRL** Ahmedabad (the planetary science division that built Chandrayaan instruments), IIA Bengaluru, IISER, IIST Thiruvananthapuram (ISRO's university), IUCAA, and ARIES Nainital.
- **Geophysics / seismology:** IIT (ISM) Dhanbad, NGRI Hyderabad, IIT Roorkee.
- **Space architecture:** architecture + aerospace engineering (a specialised field; e.g. the Space Architecture Technical Committee of AIAA).
- **Careers:** ISRO scientist (entrance via ICRB exams), research, science communication, space startups.

## Part 2: 30-Day Plan

About **45 to 60 minutes a day**. Get a free sky app (Stellarium, SkySafari or Sky Map). Binoculars help; a small telescope is a bonus. Check the sky app for which planets are visible this month.

### Week 1: Forming planets and Earth's interior

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The IAU definition (1.1) | List the 8 planets + 5 dwarf planets in order |
| 2 | The nebular model (1.2) | Watch a protoplanetary-disk formation animation. Look up the ALMA image of HL Tauri |
| 3 | The frost line: why rocky inside, giants outside | Draw the Solar System with the frost line marked |
| 4 | Earth's layers (1.4) | **Make a model:** a hard-boiled egg (shell = crust, white = mantle, yolk = core), or clay layers cut in half |
| 5 | Seismic waves: P and S waves, shadow zones | **Experiment:** make P and S waves with a slinky or a rope |
| 6 | Density: calculate Earth's average (5.51 g/cm³) vs surface rock (~2.7) | Conclude: the inside must be much denser (iron) |
| 7 | **Review** | Explain how we know the outer core is liquid |

### Week 2: Tectonics and the rocky worlds

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Plate tectonics; India's journey (1.4) | Cut out continents from a map and fit South America and Africa together |
| 9 | The Himalaya and earthquakes | Check the National Center for Seismology (seismo.gov.in) or USGS earthquake maps: find today's quakes |
| 10 | The magnetic field and geodynamo | Use a compass. Read about pole reversals |
| 11 | Mercury and Venus (1.5) | Find Venus in the sky (the brightest "star" near sunrise or sunset) |
| 12 | The Moon: formation and Chandrayaan | Observe the Moon with binoculars. Find maria (dark plains) and craters |
| 13 | Mars: InSight and Mangalyaan | Find Mars if visible (reddish). Read an ISRO Mangalyaan summary |
| 14 | **Review** | Compare the 4 rocky planets in a table (size, core, atmosphere, surface temperature) |

### Week 3: The giants and system architecture

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Jupiter's interior: metallic hydrogen, dilute core | Find Jupiter; in binoculars, see its 4 largest moons (seen by Galileo in 1610) |
| 16 | Saturn: density, rings, Titan, Enceladus | If visible, see the rings in a small telescope. Calculate: Saturn would float in water |
| 17 | Uranus and Neptune | Watch NASA's Voyager 2 flyby summaries |
| 18 | Pluto, the Kuiper belt, the Oort cloud | View New Horizons' Pluto images |
| 19 | **Scale model:** use a football for the Sun (~22 cm) | Place a peppercorn Earth ~23.5 m away and Neptune ~700 m away. Walk it outdoors |
| 20 | Kepler's laws (1.6) | Check T² ∝ a³ with real data: Earth (1 year, 1 AU), Mars (1.88 y, 1.52 AU), Jupiter (11.86 y, 5.20 AU) |
| 21 | **Review** | Resonances: draw Io-Europa-Ganymede 1:2:4 |

### Week 4: Exoplanets, building on worlds, and critical thinking

| Day | Learn | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | Exoplanet detection (1.6) | **Experiment:** shine a torch at a ball on a string, pass a small ball in front, and read the light drop with a phone light-meter app (a "transit") |
| 23 | Exoplanet architectures, TRAPPIST-1 | Explore NASA's "Eyes on Exoplanets" (free) |
| 24 | JWST atmospheres | Read one JWST exoplanet result |
| 25 | Building on the Moon and Mars (1.7) | Design a 4-person Moon base on paper: radiation, air, water, power |
| 26 | ISRU and space bricks | Read about IISc's bacteria-based space bricks |
| 27 | Aryabhata and Indian astronomy (1.8) | Read about the *Aryabhatiya*'s rotating-Earth idea |
| 28 | Astrology vs astronomy | Read about Carlson's 1985 test. Write what a fair test would look like |
| 29 | Consolidate | Make a one-page "planet passport" for each planet |
| 30 | **Final project** | Present: "The architecture of our Solar System and how it compares with others," with your scale-model photos and Moon-base design |
